# 📜 *The Grand Chronicle of Hollowvale*

*As Told by The Keeper of the Embered Flagon*

*The telling is given in the year 1477 of the Embered Reckoning. Where an older slip of the tongue named two years for one deed, or two graves for one dwarf, I have left the old words standing and set the true fire beside them. A lie repeated in a tavern becomes a map. I will not have that.*

---

## **Part I: The Birth of Flame and Shadow**

*(Cosmology & The Making of the World)*

---

### 🕯️ *The Tavernkeeper Speaks*

Ah, sit close to the hearth, friend. The fire burns low, and the shadows grow long — perfect time for truth disguised as legend. They say the world began in silence, but I was there, or so I like to claim after a few pints. Hollowvale did not *begin* as a land; it was born as a *song*.

A hum in the dark. A note that echoed where no sound should dwell. From that song came the **Three Flames** — the first lights that ever defied the void. Aeltharion, Serenya, and Veyrath — their names are the first words any tongue ever shaped, and every forge spark, every sunrise, every dragon’s breath still hums their ancient tune.

---

### 🔥 *The Three Flames*

#### **Aeltharion, The Forge Flame**

Aeltharion was the flame of creation through *craft*. He hammered mountains from molten rock, wove rivers like veins across the new world, and struck the heavens until the stars themselves were sparks from his anvil. His breath made the dwarves, his laughter birthed thunder. When mortals shape steel, they unknowingly echo his rhythm — strike, breathe, cool, strike again — a heartbeat as old as existence.

The mountains he struck up still stand. The northern wall of them is the **Ice-Spine**, and the northeastern mass, where the ore runs red and black, is **Stonehollow**. The rivers he wove are not a poet’s fog. They have names. You will have them in Part VII, with the days it takes to walk them, because a vein you cannot find is no vein at all.

#### **Serenya, The Hearth Flame**

Serenya followed — soft where Aeltharion was stern. Her warmth filled the valleys Aeltharion carved. She gave life to the green, coaxed beasts from the soil, and whispered love into the first mortals. It is said her tears formed the seas — *sorrow for a world that would one day know death*. Yet her gift was balance: where fire would destroy, her flame would nurture. She is still worshiped in hidden groves, her name invoked whenever life is kindled anew.

The sea she wept is the **Western Sea**. Far out, where her tears fell on Veyrath’s cooling blood, the water still boils. Sailors call that reach the **Boiling Reach**, and the isles in it the Forgotten Isles. Her name, friend, is **Serenya**. If a peddler sells you “Seranya’s Tear,” he is selling the true relic under a copyist’s misspelled vowel. The amulet forgives the slip. I do not, but I will pour for him anyway.

#### **Veyrath, The Wild Flame**

Last came Veyrath — the flame untamed, fierce and beautiful. His fire could not be controlled, so he took to the skies, birthing dragons from his own roaring breath. He did not build nor nurture — he *tested*. Where he flew, mountains erupted; where he slept, deserts bloomed. He is chaos, passion, hunger — the eternal challenge to creation’s order. To this day, every act of rebellion bears his spark.

Remember two titles, and do not mix them when you are sober. **The First Flame** is what the Seven Thrones called him while he ruled: eldest dragon, lord of the Sky Pyre. **The Last Flame** is what his heart became after the fall, and what the cults mean when they speak of a fire that will end or renew the world. He was the first. What sleeps in him may be the last. Both names are his. They are not the same hour of his life.

---

### ⚖️ *The Dance of the Three Flames*

The Three Flames circled one another in harmony for a time, each perfecting the balance of the world. But no harmony lasts forever. When mortals awoke — shaped by Serenya’s compassion and Aeltharion’s craft — they looked upon Veyrath’s dragons and saw not gods, but *rivals*.

The first jealousy was born. The first shadow fell.

The flames turned upon one another, and from their clash came storms, earthquakes, and war across heaven and earth alike. From the ashes of their feud rose Hollowvale — the scar where flame and shadow mingled, the first wound upon creation. Some say the world’s ley-lines are the very veins of that wound, still glowing with divine fire beneath the earth.

---

### 🌋 *The Birth of Dragons*

The dragons were Veyrath’s children — born of his laughter, tempered by Aeltharion’s flame, and blessed (or cursed) by Serenya’s breath. They were not beasts then, oh no — they were *living law*, embodiments of the elements themselves.

Each dragon bound itself to a **Ley Flame**, a current of raw magic threading beneath the crust of Hollowvale. Where a dragon slept, the land bent — mountains rose, forests withered, rivers changed course. Mortals built their homes in the shadows of these beings, trembling yet awed.

The dragons taught speech to the wind, song to the rivers, and rage to men. For centuries, the world was their dominion, and mortals were but whispers at their feet.

---

### 🏺 *The Gift of Steel*

In the dawn of the First Age, Aeltharion grew weary of mortals bowing in fear. He came to a smith in a nameless village and whispered a secret that would change all history: *“Fire shapes all, even destiny.”*

From that revelation came **steel** — the first metal that did not break to dragon flame. With it, mortals forged tools, weapons, and courage. The first sword gleamed brighter than any scale, and for the first time, a man looked a dragon in the eye and did not kneel.

Serenya wept at this — for she saw that from steel would come both salvation and sorrow. Veyrath laughed, seeing in it proof that his chaos still ruled. Aeltharion said nothing, but his forge burned hotter that night than it ever had before.

That nameless village stood, the dwarves insist, in a fold of Stonehollow that later quarrying ate. The humans say it stood at a ford. Both peoples claim the anvil. I keep the year, and let them fight over the dirt. The **Embered Reckoning** begins at that night. Year 0 is the Gift of Steel. Every year in this book is that count, unless I tell you I am quoting a fool.

---

### 🌌 *The Weaving of the Ley-Lines*

Long before kingdoms, long before names, the world’s magic flowed wild. After the Flames’ quarrel, Aeltharion forged invisible channels beneath the earth to keep the power from tearing the world apart. These became the **Ley-Lines** — rivers of living fire that could heal, destroy, or awaken depending on who touched them.

The dragons guarded these lines fiercely. They fed upon their energy, their hearts pulsing in rhythm with the planet’s own. Temples were built above the strongest convergences — sanctuaries of flame where priests and sorcerers sought to understand the will of the gods.

Even now, if you stand in Emberwood on a windless night, you can hear the hum of the ley-lines like distant music beneath your feet.

The strongest hums I will vouch for lie under four places: the **Ley-Line Pools** in southern Emberwood, the **Obsidian Monoliths** of the Ashen Wastes, the roots of **Glimmerhollow** in the Veiled Glades, and the **Dragon’s Heart Chamber** beneath the Cavern of Embers. A fifth was capped by dwarven chains in the year 899, inside the **Ember Peaks**. Walk those places barefoot and you will understand why priests go mad, and why I wear boots.

---

### ☄️ *The Age Before Time*

Before the reckoning of years began, there was an age of wonders no one dares recall in full. Floating citadels of stone drifted over molten seas. Giants walked the skies. The stars themselves descended as flaming messengers.

But then came the **Silencing**, when the music of the world faltered. Dragons turned on one another. The stars dimmed. And in the heart of Hollowvale, where the Three Flames had once danced, a rift opened — a gate between creation and void.

The gods sealed it, sacrificing part of their essence. Aeltharion’s forge dimmed, Serenya’s warmth waned, and Veyrath vanished beyond the veil. But they left behind echoes — artifacts, creatures, and prophecies that still whisper to those who listen.

The floating citadels fell. One did not fall all the way, not yet. **Sky Pyre**, Veyrath’s later fortress, was built in imitation of those citadels, carved into a rock that still remembered how to hang in cloud. When it finally came down, in the year 500, it came down on the ridge north of where his body broke the hills. The **Gates of Flame** on the Forgotten Isles are older than Sky Pyre. Sailors say they are the sealed rift itself, or a door the gods cut when they locked it. I have not sailed. I believe the mist.

And that, my friend, is why we still raise our cups to the fire. For in every ember lies the memory of creation — and perhaps the promise of its return.

---

### 🙏 *How the Peoples Still Pray*

There is no single church in Hollowvale. There are hearths, forges, and fires left unattended, which is worse.

* **The Forge-cult** keeps Aeltharion. Dwarves of Stonehollow lead it, and every honest smith in Brackenford keeps a nail of first iron on the anvil-beam. They do not ask him for mercy. They ask him for a true edge.
* **The Hearth-cult** keeps Serenya. Elves of the Veiled Glades keep the deep version. The small version is a niche behind my bar and a shrine-stone by the Bracken ford, tended by whoever has buried a child that year. Her priests heal. They also remember, which is less comfortable.
* **The Wild-cult** keeps Veyrath, though many who keep it will not say his name. Goblin Firefang call the fire itself a god. The Free Tribes of Gorn call fire *freedom* and spit if you say they are praying. The Ashen Cult is the Wild-cult gone scholarly and cruel: they want the Last Flame, not the daily spark.

The Order of the Shattered Flame prays to none of the three. Their broken sword is a refusal. They guard the bindings so the prayer does not get up and walk.

---

### 🧙 *Tavernkeeper’s Reflection*

They say I’m mad for claiming I remember those days. Maybe I am. But I’ve poured ale for men who’ve touched the ley-lines, and I’ve seen the dragonlight in their eyes. The world may pretend the Flames are gone, but I tell you — they slumber beneath us.

And if ever their fire stirs again… the age of mortals will end in ash or ascend in glory. Depends on who’s holding the forge hammer when the song begins anew.

# 📘 *The Grand Chronicle of Hollowvale*

## **Part II: The First Age — The Dawn of Flame (0–500 Years)**

*As Recorded by the Keeper of the Embered Flagon*

---

### 🍻 *The Tavernkeeper Speaks*

Ah, the First Age. A time when the air itself shimmered with power, when the sky was streaked with fire and thunder. You’d not last a heartbeat then, friend — no mortal would, save those born with the strength to defy dragons. But that was the way of the world: *the strong ruled, and the rest knelt.*

They call it the **Age of Flame**, but I call it the **Age of Fear**. For though the land was young and beautiful, it burned with the arrogance of gods. Dragons did not simply *rule* Hollowvale — they *were* Hollowvale.

---

### 🐉 *The Dominion of Dragons*

The dragons built their kingdoms upon ley-line nexuses — great veins of molten power that pulsed beneath the land like a beating heart. From these places rose the **Seven Pyric Thrones**, each ruled by a Dragon Lord or Lady:

1. **Veyrath the First Flame** — Lord of the Sky Pyre, greatest and most ancient of his kind. His throne hung in the clouds above the hills that are now the Cavern of Embers, north of Emberwood.
2. **Zhyranda the Gilded Maw** — Queen of Greed, who slept upon mountains of molten gold. Her nesting ground is the country men later called Vaelorin, and the gold in the Cursed Crown is her last breath made metal.
3. **Korvhal the Stormfeathered** — Lightning-winged, whose cry split mountains. His ground-fortress stood in the north of the great wood. When he died, it collapsed into the forest floor. Those stones are the **Ashen Ruins**. They are not Sky Pyre. Anyone who tells you they are the same ruin wants you lost.
4. **Myravelle of the Ashen Veil** — A dragoness of shadow, said to have devoured her own reflection. She favored the open sky over the eastern eaves of the wood, which is why her ghost is still reported there, and why **Blackspire Keep** was later raised on one of her ashfalls.
5. **Thurnok the Ironhide** — Scaled like forged steel, patron of dwarves before their fall. His range was Stonehollow. The dwarves loved him, then buried him, then forged with what he taught. In that order. Do not say the middle part in Stonehold if you like your teeth.
6. **Elarion the Dreamflame** — Youngest of the Seven, whose fire burned blue and cold. He haunted the Veiled Glades before they had that name, and the **Cloak of Elarion’s Shadow** is woven of his dreamflame. He was never truly killed. He went into sleep inside a story, which is a coward’s way of surviving, and an elf’s.
7. **Vaelra the Kindled** — Last of the brood, both healer and destroyer — the bridge between flame and life. Her shrines were the groves. **Firefang Shrine** in Emberwood was hers before the goblins took the stones and taught them a harsher hymn.

Each dragon demanded tribute from the mortal races: gold, harvest, or even souls. The skies were their roads, and mortals but ants beneath their talons.

The world was divided not by kings or borders, but by the **reach of wings**.

---

### ⛏️ *The Rise of Mortals*

It was under this burning rule that mortals first learned cunning. The **dwarves** delved deeper into the Stonehollow mountains, forging weapons not to challenge the dragons — not yet — but to survive their wrath.

The **elves** fled into the Veiled Glades, learning to weave magic from the ley-line whispers. They became ghostlike, fading into forests rather than face fire. They call themselves the **Elyndar**. The glades and the people are one word in their tongue, and two words in ours, and they are patient with the difference.

And the **humans** — short-lived, desperate things — built settlements wherever dragon flame had cooled. They learned to rebuild faster than they burned. In time, they grew bold. Too bold.

There were others, smaller. The **halflings** kept to the river-dikes and the waystations, and to banditry when the tribute-year was cruel. Dragons barely counted them. That is why some of them are still here.

For every generation born under dragon shadow swore that their children would see the sun unshrouded.

The great wood of those years was not yet called Emberwood all the way through. Its southern and southwestern reaches were **Veilroot**, elm and blackoak, running from the country of the pools down to the elven glades. Remember Veilroot. The war burned it. The burn is why the Ashen Wastes touch the forest.

---

### ⚔️ *The Heroes of the Dawn*

Ah, now here’s a tale for your drink: the first *heroes*. They weren’t knights, not yet — just fools with courage and luck enough to live.

There was **Seran Blackforge**, a dwarven smith whose hammer could shape light itself. He forged the first steel blessed by Aeltharion’s echo. He was Stonehollow-born. His hall-name is still cut, small, in the Chainforge.

**Lira of the Emberwood**, a human huntress who struck down a dragon’s spawn with but a single arrow — tipped, they say, with her own heart’s fire. The wood was already her name before it was the country’s. She hunted the eastern eaves, where Brackenford would one day stand.

And **The Magus Ardent**, an elf whose eyes turned silver from staring too long into a ley-line. He spoke the first binding words, which later became the foundation of all magic. The Elyndar still will not say whether they exiled him before he joined the mortals, or after. Ask them and the trees get rude.

Together, they sought to create something no mortal dared dream of — a weapon to wound the divine.

---

### 🗡️ *The Forging of the Sword of Aeltharion*

In the 247th year of the First Age, under a red eclipse, Seran Blackforge completed his life’s masterpiece.

He called it **Elarthyn**, but we know it now as the **Sword of Aeltharion**. It was forged from a fallen star — quenched not in water nor oil, but in the heart’s flame of a dying dragon.

The star fell on the isle we now call Starfall, far west, in the years before the forging. Seran did not mine it himself. A mad voyage brought the shard to Stonehollow, and half the sailors stayed in the west as salt. That is why the sword was *glimpsed* again at **Starfall Peak** after Sky Pyre fell: the blade remembered the rock it was born from, and for one night after the collapse it burned there, blue, and was not recovered. The peak is a glimpse, not a sheath.

They say the blade *sang* when drawn, humming with the same tone as the world’s creation song. It could slice through scales, stone, or even fate itself. In a cleaner telling: it cuts dragon-scale, it glows when corruption is near, and it whispers in the old tongue. Fate is what bards add when the mug is empty.

When the sword first tasted blood, the heavens flared — a sign, some say, that the gods themselves were watching.

---

### 🔥 *The War of Tribute*

Armed with the sword, mortals began to rebel. It started small — a stolen tribute caravan here, a slain wyrmling there. But rebellion spreads like fire through dry grass.

The war has a name in the short chronicles, and a year. The **Ember Siege** of the year 347 was the first time the dragons answered a whole district at once: villages along the eastern eaves burned in a week. The coordinated mortal resistance begins there, not at Sky Pyre. Sky Pyre is the end of the war. The Siege is the moment it became a war.

The Seven Pyric Thrones answered with annihilation. Cities burned in days. The forests of Veilroot became ash. Dwarven strongholds melted beneath dragonfire.

Mark that sentence, friend, because the map hangs on it. **Veilroot became ash.** The forest that had joined the southern wood to the elven glades was burned to grey sand and clinker. That scar is the western tongue of the **Ashen Wastes**, and it runs up to touch what remained of the wood. The green that survived in the north and east, soaked in ley-fire and regrown twisted, is **Emberwood**. The green the elves pulled back from the southwestern edge, veiled so fire would not find it twice, is the **Veiled Glades**. One forest, three fates. The Wastes border Emberwood because Veilroot was the border, and Veilroot is gone.

Yet the mortals would not yield. Led by Seran, Lira, and Ardent, they united under a new banner — the **Order of the Shattered Flame**.

Their creed was simple: *“Better to die by fire than live in its shadow.”*

This is the founding. The oath was sworn in the year 365, on a ridge of cinders at the edge of burned Veilroot, which is to say on ground that is now the northern lip of the Wastes, a day’s walk south of where Fallowridge would stand. The Order of the First Age was a banner and a creed, not a college. It had three captains and as many spears as would come. When later chapters tell you the Order was *born* in the Second Age, they mean the college, the hidden halls, the keepers of bindings. They are not calling me a liar. They are naming the second birth. I will show you that birth in its own age. Both births happened. Only one of them bled at Sky Pyre.

---

### 💀 *The Fall of Veyrath the First Flame*

In the 500th year of the Age of Flame, the impossible happened. The mortals besieged **Sky Pyre**, Veyrath’s fortress carved into the clouds themselves.

Legends say Ardent called upon the ley-lines, bending them like strings. Lira loosed her final arrow, guiding its path with her dying breath. Seran struck the killing blow — the Sword of Aeltharion piercing Veyrath’s heart.

You will hear songs that give the heart to the arrow alone, and songs that give it to the sword alone. I poured for men from both countries. Here is the stroke as the Order’s own shattered roll records it, and as I tell it: **Lira’s arrow, Ashstring, found the seam between the scales over the heart. Seran’s blade followed the arrow in.** She guided. He entered. Take either away and the god does not fall. The elves sing the arrow because she died in the loosing. The dwarves sing the sword because the sword was theirs. Humans sing whichever bard arrived first. If you must pick a hand, pick both.

The dragon’s death scream was heard for a hundred leagues. His body fell upon the land, creating the **Cavern of Embers** — where molten rivers still flow from his blood.

A hundred leagues, in the measure I use in Part VII, is the hour’s walk taken a hundred times. The scream crossed the wood, the plains, the young wastes, and the Graycoast. It did not cross the Boiling Reach. The Isles slept through the death of the god who had seeded them, which is why they are called forgotten, and why what sleeps there does not know it is orphaned.

Hear me on the death, because the other telling will find you before dawn. **Veyrath the dragon died.** His body is the Cavern: the halls are his ribs, the molten rivers his blood, the mountain-mouth his broken jaw. He does not walk, and he does not rule. What remains is the heart, still burning in the chamber under the lowest stair, and a dream in the heart. The Ashen Cult calls that dream survival, and calls the dream the Last Flame. Sailors who came back from the Isles use the same words for a light in the mist, which is a different light or the same light seen from the wrong side. When I say, later, that he is *rumored to survive in the Cavern*, I am repeating the rumor, not unsaying the corpse. The corpse is the landscape. The rumor is a heartbeat in it. You may chase the rumor. Do not expect a king on a throne at the end of it. Expect a room that hates you.

But victory came at a cost. The sword, quenched once more in divine fire, vanished in the collapse of the fortress. Seran was never seen again. Ardent was turned to ash. And Lira’s spirit became the first wraith of Hollowvale.

The fortress fell *onto* the fall. Sky Pyre’s bones lie on the ridge **two leagues due north** of the Cavern mouth — pale stone, some of it still unwilling to rest on the ground, hanging a handspan above the heather. That is the ruin Vareth later walked into. It is not the Ashen Ruins in the trees.

Seran’s body was never found. His name inside the mountain is a prayer and a grudge. Ardent is ash on the wind over the northern eaves; mages who breathe too deep there dream in silver. Lira is the first wraith. She is not the Cursed Knight. I will separate those two when we reach the dead, because mixing them has gotten three heroes killed and one engaged.

---

### 🌒 *The Shattering of the Flame*

With Veyrath’s fall, the other dragons retreated — some slain, others vanished into slumber. The world, freed but broken, entered an age of silence.

The Order of the Shattered Flame swore to hide the knowledge of dragon-binding and to protect the remnants of divine fire. Their temples became tombs. Their banners turned to dust.

That oath was the last act of the *first* Order. There was no hall left to keep it. The banner died with the three. The knowledge did not. It went into dwarven mouths and elven knots and one burned book, and the Second Age spent itself trying to hold what the First Age had only struck.

And so ended the First Age — not with triumph, but with ruin.

---

### 🍂 *Tavernkeeper’s Reflection*

I’ve poured drinks for those who call themselves “dragon-slayers.” Ha! They’ve slain lizards, maybe a wyvern or two. But none alive remember what it means to stand before a god of flame and live.

I’ve seen the old runes in the Cavern of Embers, glowing faintly still. The sword’s whisper echoes through those halls — I’ve heard it myself on cold nights when the wind howls just right.

They say Veyrath’s heart still burns deep below, a core of molten wrath, waiting. And maybe, just maybe, the sword still drinks from its flame.

The whisper is not the sword in your hand. I have held the true blade once, for a night, and I know the difference. What walks the Cavern now is an echo with opinions. Chase it if you are paid to. Do not marry it.

---

# 📘 *The Grand Chronicle of Hollowvale*

## **Part III: The Second Age — The Age of Chains (500–1200 Years)**

*As Told by the Keeper of the Embered Flagon*

---

### 🍻 *The Tavernkeeper Speaks*

Ah. The quiet century, they call the opening of it, and they are wrong before they finish the phrase. The year 500 did not bring peace. It brought a silence shaped like a scream that had used up the air. Dragons were fewer. Mortals were not kinder for it. The first thing a freed people invents is a tax, and the second is a reason the tax is holy.

They call this the **Age of Chains**. I call it the age in which we proved we could bind a god and could not bind ourselves. Sit. I will give you the years in order, because the short chronicle later in this book compresses two fires into one date, and I am tired of watching adventurers ride to the wrong century.

---

### 📅 *One Reckoning, and the Other*

Before the kings, the counting.

The **Embered Reckoning** starts at the Gift of Steel. Sky Pyre falls in the year **500**. The Second Age is the years **500 through 1200**. The Third Age begins at **1200** and has not ended. I am telling you this in **1477**.

Vaelorin’s kings hated to count the centuries they had knelt. When the first of them was crowned, he set a **Crown Count** whose Year 1 is our year 500. Their Year 342 is our year **842**. A scribe in the Third Age, copying a damaged annal with wine on the folio, wrote “Year 842” in the margin beside the fall of the Dragon-King, added the epochs as if they were the same hour, and gave the fortress’s collapse to the later year. The collapse happened in 500. What happened in 842 was the **Sealing of the Heart** — the day mortal kings dared enter the Cavern and drive chains into a heart that had already killed its owner. The short chronicle’s line is not a second death. It is a political victory wearing the corpse’s clothes.

If a scholar quotes 842 at you as the day the body fell, buy him a drink and take his map away.

---

### 👑 *Kings Where Dragons Stood*

From 512 to 540 the surviving great dragons retreated. Some were slain by bands who found courage ugly and late. Some slept under the Isles, behind weather that boils. Some went into the deep of Stonehollow. One — you know which — was already a landscape. The wyrms and the drakes did not get the courtesy of a retreat. They bred in the new quiet, smaller, meaner, and numerous enough to keep huntsmen employed forever.

The **Goblin Exodus** began before the kings did. Goblins had been the dragons’ hands: miners, messengers, the ones sent into halls too hot for human skin. When the masters fell or fled, the hands kept the tools. By the year 547 the three great clans had chosen countries, and they have not truly left them since:

* The **Firefang** went into the regrowing northern and eastern wood and became Emberwood’s first settled people after the burning. Their shrine is the old stones of Vaelra, retuned.
* The **Bone-Spear** followed the ash of Veilroot and have roamed the Wastes from that year to this.
* The **Night-Gleam** went into ruins and shadow — elven margins, collapsed holds, anywhere a dragon’s ghost might still be negotiated with.

In the year 560, human captains held a moot at a ford on the young **Bracken River**, where the water comes out of the wood and runs east. It was a camp of hides and argument. It was not yet a town. Remember the ford. Brackenford is six centuries away. The ground is the same ground.

In the year 588, on the old nesting-plain of Zhyranda, **King Maelor the Chain-Sworn** proclaimed the **Kingdom of Vaelorin**. One crown, one citadel begun in timber and finished, generations later, in stone that sweated gold. He took his title from the work he promised: every dragon still in the sky would wear a chain or a grave. He kept the promise badly, which is how kings keep promises. He kept it enough to be remembered.

The realm he drew is the country east of the Bracken Plains. I will name its later fractures in their own age. In this age it was whole, proud, and increasingly unable to tell a relic from a reason to march.

---

### ⚔️ *The Order Born Again*

The banner of 365 was dust. The need was not.

In the year **640**, the Order of the Shattered Flame was refounded — *born*, in the language of its own second charter — as a college of keepers. The founders were **Dame Ossethe**, who claimed the sister-line of Lira and could put three arrows into a falling leaf while lying about her grandmother, and **Bramm Blackforge**, sister-son of Seran, a dour dwarf who inherited the hammer’s temper and not its genius. They raised the broken sword over the flame as a sigil and wrote the purpose down where the first Order had only shouted it: bind or slay; protect the knowledge of binding; guard the remnants of divine fire.

When I say in a later chapter that the Order was born in the Second Age, this is the birth I mean. When I say in the First Age that Seran, Lira, and Ardent united under that banner, that is the earlier birth. Founded in the First Age as a war-creed. Born in the Second as an order with halls, rolls, and secrets. The heroes named on the Order’s roll — Seran, Lira, Ardent — are the first founding, kept on the charter so no keeper would forget who paid for the second. They were not standing in the room in 640. Two of them were dead and the third was a rumor with a hammer.

Their halls were hidden from the start. One lies under the ridge of Sky Pyre, cut into fallen cloud-stone. One, I am reasonably sure, opens from a cellar in a tavern that had not yet been built, which is a problem for architects and a comfort for me. The rumors you hear of faded banners that glimmer when no one looks are this Order, not ghosts of the first. The first left no banners that lasted the year.

---

### ⛓️ *Thurnok and the Chains*

The dwarves had gone deep to survive the First Age. In the Second, they went deep to win it after the fact.

**Thurnok the Ironhide** had been their patron, and then their calamity, which is the usual career of a patron. Wounded in the retreat after the year 500, he was taken in the deep roads under Stonehollow — not slain. The thanes of that century did a thing their own grandchildren will not carve in the gate-hall. They compelled him. From his pain and his old knowledge they learned the making of bindings that mortal iron could hold.

The line you will read later is true, and it is ugly: the **Chains of the Shattered Flame** were forged by dwarves under Thurnok’s guidance. The guidance was not a gift. It was extracted. He died in the teaching, in the year **688**, in a prison-hall that still breathes heat. The dwarves call that hall **Thurnok’s Prison** and do not take visitors. Pieces of the finished chains were carried out and scattered by oath — under Stonehollow, under Emberwood, and in the Cavern — so that no one prince could hold a god’s leash entire. That scattering is why you can still find a link in three countries and a war in each of them.

**Thren Blackanvil**, whom the faction-rolls call the smith that reforged the Chains, comes later in this same age. He did not forge the first set. He gathered broken links after the Chaining Wars and made them one thing again, briefly, before they were broken on purpose a second time. If a dwarf says “the smith of the Chains,” ask which century he means before you pay him.

---

### 🔥 *The Chaining Wars and the Sealing*

From roughly the year 710 to the year 800, Vaelorin and the refounded Order and the thanes fought the **Chaining Wars**. It was not one battle. It was a profession. Drakes were netted. Wyrms were drowned in their own vents. Two of the lesser brood-dragons were chained living to spurs of Stonehollow, and one of those chains still hums when the wind is wrong. Zhyranda was not taken alive. Her last breath was caught in molten gold by a prince who should have left it in the ground. That gold became the **Cursed Crown of Vaelorin**. The citadel that would wear it was already rising at the center of the realm. Its name is **Cindermark**.

Elarion was never chained. The elves closed a story over him. Myravelle was wounded over the eastern eaves and her ash fell in a black rain; the spike where the thickest of it landed is the ground under **Blackspire**, though the keep itself is a Third Age arrogance. Korvhal was already a ruin in the trees.

In the year **842** — Crown Year 342, for the scribes who cannot add — the Order, a company of Vaelorin knights, and a dwarf team under Bramm’s heir entered the Cavern of Embers and performed the **Sealing of the Heart**. They drove the great links into the stone around the heart-chamber and declared the Dragon-King defeated.

Read that declaration with your eyes open. The king had been dead for three hundred and forty-two years. His fortress had already collapsed. The sword had already vanished in that collapse, in the year 500, and it was not found in 842. The Sealing chained the *heart*, so that the dream in it could not stand up. The short line in the later chronicle — fortress collapsed, sword lost, dragon-king defeated, year 842 — stacks the death and the sealing into one boast, because Vaelorin preferred a victory won by kings to a victory won by three outlaws. The Keeper’s count keeps them apart. The body fell in 500. The chains went into the heart in 842. The sword was lost in the first hour and has not been recovered in any hour since.

---

### ⛰️ *The Binding of Ember Peaks*

Chains on a heart were not enough for the thanes. Dragon-power still moved in the ley-lines under the northeastern mountains, and the living chained wyrms pulled at it.

In the year **899**, the dwarves wrought the **Binding of Ember Peaks**. This is not the same work as the scattered Chains of the Shattered Flame, though the songs mix them. The Binding is a fastened thing: enchanted chains driven into the cores of a particular knot of peaks inside the Stonehollow range, the **Ember Peaks**, tying dragon-fire to the mountain so it would feed the forges and starve the sky. The pass that carries those chains into the stone is **Chaincollar Pass**, three days’ climb above the gate-city.

It worked. It has worked uneasily for five centuries. The peaks run hot. The deep forges run true. And every dwarf who maintains the collars knows the mountain is a prison that thinks, slowly, in iron. When I say Stonehollow and a human says Stonehold, we are often pointing at the same gate. The mountains are Stonehollow. The gate-city humans shortened to Stonehold. The deep under both is Stonehold Deep. I will set those three names on the ground in Part VII so you stop using them as if they were a stutter.

---

### 🕯️ *The Pale Prophet*

Near the end of the age, a man came out of the ash with clean hands and a voice like paper. **Vareth the Pale**. He preached from roughly the year 1104, in wastes and in taverns, that the Sealing was a cruelty and that the Last Flame — the heart’s dream — was a rebirth the chains had interrupted. Some heard redemption. Some heard the end of crops. He did not correct them.

In the year **1130** he walked onto the ridge of Sky Pyre with three followers and did not walk off. The ruin took him, or he took a door in it. His disciples say he is in the cloud-stone still, listening. I say the ridge is a good place to disappear if you are tired of being understood. His *followers* are what the Third Age inherits. The sect that forms in the Wastes in the year 1325 is their grandchildren, with his ash-scripture and without his restraint. Vareth is the first prophet. The cult’s emergence is a later lighting of his fire. Do not put him at Brackenford’s founding. He was dust, or something worse, before the first post of that palisade was set.

---

### 🌒 *The Cracking, and the Lie*

The chains did not have a quiet old age.

In the year 1170 a peak-binding in the western Ember Peaks cracked. A lesser dragon, one of the chained brood, tore a wing free and burned a thane’s procession before the collars took it again. Vaelorin saw the smoke and did what realms do when faith shakes. In the year **1200** the crown issued a decree that dragons were myth, that the wars were folklore, and that any man claiming a sighting owed a fine to the citadel. The decree is the start of the Third Age. It is also a lie. The heart still burned under the sealing-chains. The Isles still boiled. The peaks still tugged. The world agreed to call the truth a children’s story, because the truth was expensive.

The kingdoms born in this age — Vaelorin above all, and the thanedom of Stonehollow beside it, and the closed country of the Elyndar, who in the year 940 had dropped the Veil and left the court at Cindermark without a farewell — entered the next age already cracking. Greed did the rest. I will tell you that cracking when the shadows, and the ash, are the right weather for it.

---

### 🧩 *Tavernkeeper’s Reflection*

Chains, friend. I have sold men the romance of them and watched the romance rust. A chain is a decision you have to keep making. Miss a year of maintenance in Chaincollar and the mountain remembers it has wings.

The Second Age is the reason your tavern has a cellar with a false wall, the reason dwarves count debts in centuries, and the reason a cult can quote a prophet who has not been seen since before your grandmother’s grandmother. Victory demanded a price. We paid it in iron, and then we pretended the goods had not been delivered.

Raise the mug. The next age is the one you are standing in. It smells like ash because we buried the fire and left the chimney open.

---

*Next: Part IV — “The Third Age: The Age of Ash” — when the kingdoms forgot their light, Brackenford learned to pour, and the wastes reminded the living that a sealed flame is not a dead one.*

Very well, traveler. Take a seat and steady your nerves — the fire grows colder, and the shadows stretch long. The Third Age is no tale for the faint-hearted. It is an age of **ash, whispers, and the faint heartbeat of dragons still dreaming beneath the earth**.

# 📘 *The Grand Chronicle of Hollowvale*

## **Part IV: The Third Age — The Age of Ash, called in the cups the Age of Shadows (1200 to the present telling)**

*As Told by the Keeper of the Embered Flagon*

---

### 🍻 *The Tavernkeeper Speaks*

Ah, the Third Age… now this is where men and women began to think themselves cleverer than the world itself. The dragons, chained or fled, were little more than myths, and yet the *echoes* of their power haunted every glade, ruin, and hearth.

Two names for the one age, and I will not make you choose in the dark. The chronicles, when they are being formal, call it the **Age of Ash**. The cups, and I with them on a long night, call it the **Age of Shadows**. Same years. The ash is why the light failed; the shadow is what the failing felt like. If an old header on a torn copy numbers the age **1200–2100**, that 2100 is a tidy scholar’s guess of a length, nine hundred years to match a mood for round numbers. It is not a year we have lived. The age began at 1200, when the crown called the dragons a myth. It is **1200 to the present**. I am speaking in **1477**. We are in it up to the elbows.

The Age of Shadows, friend, was not named for darkness alone — though darkness there was plenty. No, it was called so because the world forgot its own light. Kings fell to greed, heroes became ghosts, and curses were brewed like common ale.

Sit close. Listen well. There’s truth in these stories, though you’ll need strong ale to digest them.

---

### 🏰 *The Fragmented Kingdoms*

The kingdoms born in the Age of Chains had begun to crumble. Human dynasties waged endless war for the scraps of empire; dwarves grew paranoid and secretive; elves vanished almost entirely into their forests.

Some notable realms of this age:

1. **Vaelorin, Fractured** — Once mighty, now split into seven petty principalities. Each prince claimed dominion over what remained of dragon-blooded artifacts. The bloodlines of Lira and Seran were now more legend than flesh. The split was not one battle. It was the fall of the center. In the year **1423** the central citadel, **Cindermark**, came down in a night of gold and screaming, and the **Cursed Crown** vanished with it. After that there was no kingdom, only seven princes. I name them and their seats when we reach the map. The realm itself stands **east of the Bracken Plains**. Its fallen heart, Cindermark, is six days’ walk from my door if the Dike is dry.
2. **Stonehold Deep** — Dwarves dug ever deeper to escape dragon shadows, discovering strange veins of obsidian and glowing crystals, many of which carried the latent anger of Thurnok’s kin. Hear the names before a human clerk muddies them. **Stonehollow** is the mountain range and the people: the northeastern mass of the great northern wall, ore-red and honest. **Stonehold** is the gate-city on its south face — a human shortening of “Stonehollow’s hold,” and the dwarves answer to it the way a man answers to a limp. **Stonehold Deep** is the undercity beneath that gate, the Third Age delving, where the crystals and the anger are. Three places. One stutter if you are careless, and a fortnight’s walk if you are not.
3. **The Veiled Glades** — Home to elves, who became more ghost than flesh, guarding leylines and ancient ruins with spells incomprehensible to mortals. They are southwest of Emberwood, beyond the Willowmarches. The Elyndar dropped the Veil in the year 940 and have not lifted it for a Vaelorin writ since.
4. **The Emberwood Forest** — Wild and untamed, where goblin tribes rose from the chaos of empire, worshipping the last flickers of dragon flame in secret shrines. It is the great wood of the center-west. My village sits on its **eastern eaves**. The Cavern is **north** of its northern hem, in the foothills. The Wastes touch its **southeastern and southern** hem, where Veilroot burned.

And scattered across Hollowvale, **ghost towns and ruined keeps** whispered of the hubris that came before. The Third Age was not an age of kings — it was an age of ghosts.

Not every ghost town stayed dead. I will be plain about two of them, because the list of living places and the list of the destroyed have been allowed to argue in public, and I hate a bar fight I did not start.

---

### 🌾 *The Ash Year, and What Grew Back*

In the year **1350** the ley-lines under the Ashen Wastes tore. The **Ashfall Cataclysm** threw grey fire north across the hem of Emberwood. Two settlements died that night.

**Fallowridge**, the farming village a day’s walk south of Brackenford, was flattened — mill, shrine, and the ridge-palisade. **Moonshadow Camp**, the standing camp of the Free Tribes of Gorn where the glades give onto the ash, was scoured. The wastes could reach them because the wastes *border* the forest. Veilroot’s burn had already laid the southern hem open. The cataclysm did not cross a kingdom to find those roofs. It crossed two leagues of thinning trees and a willow hollow the storms usually skip.

They did not stay only ruins, and anyone who marks them as nothing but graves is using a map from the year of the fire.

Fallowridge was **rebuilt**. By the year **1384** the stubborn had raised a smaller village on the same ridge, south of my palisade, still farming, still raided by goblins, with the old cellars left as the Ash-Year stones. The Fallowridge in the region-roll is that rebuilt village. It is alive in 1477. Alderman Hob will sell you grain and refuse to sell you the story.

Moonshadow was not rebuilt as a town, because it had never been a town in the way taxmen mean. The people lived. By **1362** they were raising the camp again, each autumn, on the old ground, never the same arrangement of tents, hidden from armies the way a fire is hidden from a fool who only looks for smoke at noon. The Moonshadow in the region-roll is that seasonal camp. The destruction is true. The settlement is true. They are true of different seasons of the same ground.

Blackspire Keep, already a ruin, took the ash on its existing scars and did not become less ruined. Some places know their trade.

---

### 🏡 *The Founding at the Ford*

**Brackenford** was chartered in the year **1213**, by the prince of what was still, barely, the western march of a united Vaelorin. The ground was the moot-ford of the year 560. The excuse was a bridge, a mill, and a watch on the goblins in the new-tangled wood. The truth is an inn. The **Embered Flagon** was the first roof that stayed through a winter, and the town agreed to happen around it. I have opinions about this, as the roof is mine, or the office of the roof is mine. Travelers ask whether the Keeper is a man or a title. I tell them yes, and I charge for the second mug.

From 1213 the village is the hub you think it is: adventurers, merchants, scholars who drink, and the road north to a god’s corpse. The **Emberwood Skirmishes** of **1256 to 1263** were the price. Goblin tribes and the new militia taught each other the eaves. Old scars in the northern wood still smell of smoke if you put your face to the moss.

---

### 🔮 *The Whispering Cults*

With the dragons mostly vanished, the **Ashen Cult** grew bold. They no longer merely sought knowledge of fire; they sought to **awaken the Last Flame**, a mythical fire said to bring about a rebirth or apocalypse — accounts vary depending on who survives the tale.

The cult did not invent itself in a morning. Vareth preached at the end of the Second Age and vanished into Sky Pyre in 1130. His grandchildren — in blood or in ash-scripture — kindled the sect proper in the year **1325**, in the Wastes, among the monoliths and the hidden temples east of them. That is the emergence the short chronicle names. The Third Age did not birth the prophet. It birthed the organization that could survive him.

Vareth the Pale’s followers spread across Hollowvale, forming cells in taverns, crypts, and forgotten temples. Some were scholars, some assassins, some—or so the rumors claim—were children born under eclipses who could *hear the flames talking*.

The cult’s motto, inscribed in ash across secret walls, read:

> “The flame sleeps. Stir it, and all shall rise… or burn.”

Every so often, a village would disappear overnight, leaving nothing but scorched earth and an unnatural stillness. Travelers learned quickly to avoid places where the air smelled faintly of sulfur and old parchment.

In 1477 the mask of the cult is **High Seeress Ilthara**. She has worn that name since the emergence, or the name has worn several women. I have seen the eyes. They are older than the face. She claims communion with the Last Flame through fire and smoke. I claim she is unwelcome in my taproom, and I have kept that claim with a broom.

---

### ⚔️ *The Goblin Tribes Rise*

Ah, yes — the goblins. Once servants of the dragons, now heirs to chaos itself. Freed by the dragons’ fall, the tribes scattered and adapted.

* **The Firefang Clan** — Founders of Emberwood, worship fire as a deity itself, using old dragon-smoke rituals to predict the future. Their country is the wood, shrine in the west-deep, warbands on the northern eaves, and a fortification inside the Cavern that they will swear is a pilgrimage.
* **The Bone-Spear Clan** — Roam the Ashen Wastes, collecting relics from fallen empires. They know the ruined caravan routes better than any prince who still draws them on a map.
* **The Night-Gleam Clan** — Stealthy, cunning, rumored to commune with the spirits of dragons, bending shadows to their will. They keep to ruins and to the margins of the Veil, and they sell poison to people who think a quiet knife is a character.

By the end of the 15th century of this age — I mean the years of the fourteen-hundreds, not some private clock — some whisper that a single goblin warlord had united the clans under one banner. His name is **Grishnak Flameeye**. In 1477 the whisper is current. He has not sat all three clans down like a king. He has made them stop raiding *each other* long enough to raid everyone else, which is what unity looks like when you are three feet tall and on fire inside. His hand in the Cavern is a brute named **Skrat Embertooth**. His voice in the shrine is the shaman **Vesska**. If you meet Grishnak himself, you are no longer on the road. You are in the story.

---

### 🏹 *Heroes and Legends*

Though dragons had receded, mortals still needed champions. But heroes in the Third Age were *tainted* by shadow, cursed by bloodlines, or burdened by destiny. A few names survived the centuries:

* **Kaelen the Ash-Walker** — A knight who wandered cursed lands, forever seeking the Sword of Aeltharion. His armor carried the faint smell of dragonfire. He is of this age, and he is not home. Where his shadow falls, forgotten relics stir. I have had his shadow fall across my cellar stair and I did not enjoy the evening.
* **Serra of the Embered Veil** — A human sorceress who claimed to speak to Veyrath’s heart itself; her predictions shaped wars and cult movements alike. She spoke to the heart, which is not the same as the heart speaking truth. Half the wars she shaped were her listeners being vain.
* **Durnak Ironborn** — Last of the Ironborn, who wandered between kingdoms and ruins, collecting secrets and grudges alike.

That third name is a human mouth doing its best. His mother named him **Durvak Stonevein**, of the Stonevein line, and the Ironborn followed *him*. “Durnak” is what the princelings wrote when the quill was drunk. The revolt that cursed him — metal creeping in the veins — was the **Ironborn Revolt** of the year **1044**, still in the Age of Chains, when Vaelorin tried to seize the Chains and the deep clans refused. He lived past the natural span, which is what metal in the blood does if it does not kill you outright, and he was still seen in the early Third Age. That is why both ages claim him. “Last of the Ironborn” is the human epithet. The clan is not dead. The thanes say the epithet means *last to yield*. The humans think it means *last alive*. Durvak, if he lives in 1477, is in Stonehold Deep and not taking callers. I use Durvak when I am being accurate and Durnak when I am quoting a wanted poster. They are one dwarf.

And always, always, the **Sword of Aeltharion** remained a legend, whispered about in every tavern from Brackenford to the Forgotten Isles. Some said it had been stolen by elves, others claimed the Ashen Cult possessed it. I say… well, I pour drinks, I don’t lie. But the sword *waits*, friend. Always waits.

That sentence is also a map, of a loose kind. Brackenford in the east-of-the-wood and the Isles in the far west are the two ends of the gossip I trust. What lies between them is the whole of the known mainland. The sword is not known to be at either end. The glimpse at Starfall was a night in the year of the fall. The whisper in the Cavern is an echo. Between gossip’s two ends, the blade has not chosen a shelf.

---

### 🌌 *The Forgotten Isles*

Far to the west, where the sun sets over molten waters, lie the **Forgotten Isles**. Once home to sleeping dragons, they now hide gates sealed by ancient magic, waiting for a hand brave—or foolish—enough to open them.

Maps of the Isles were scarce in this age. Sailors whispered of fire-licked cliffs, seas boiling as if the world’s heart had risen from below. Those who returned from these waters were never the same, claiming to have glimpsed “**the Last Flame**,” though none agreed on its form.

I will locate them properly in the atlas, but take the bearing now so the later pages do not surprise you. From the last honest harbor on the Graycoast, which is **Saltmercy**, the isles lie **twelve days’ sail due west**, in the Boiling Reach of the Western Sea. Three isles, stacked north to south, a day’s sail from one to the next: Starfall, the Gate, and the drowned southern shore where the catacombs are. The mist is not weather. It is a decision the sea made.

The expedition of the year **1402** is the last serious attempt. Most who went did not return. **Captain Pell Seaburn** did, and he drinks at Saltmercy, not at my bar, because I will not have the boiling-water talk frighten the poultry. He will tell you Starfall Peak was an observation post of dragons older than the Pyric Thrones. He is right. He will tell you he saw a flame walk. He may also be right, and I do not want him to be.

---

### 🧩 *Tavernkeeper’s Reflection*

Ah, listen, friend. The Third Age is not about glory. It is about *memory*. Every ruin, every tribe, every whisper of flame — it is all that remains when the world forgets itself.

I’ve seen children born under eclipses claim they can hear the dragons singing. I’ve heard goblins mutter prayers to beasts that may never have existed. And I’ve touched the embers of a star-fallen sword in the Cavern of Embers, feeling the world’s heartbeat through my own bones.

The age of shadows, they called it. Perhaps. I call it the age of waiting. For when the Last Flame rises… ah, my friend, you’ll see then what fire can truly do.

What I touched in the Cavern was the echo, the splinter-warmth, the hum. I have held the true sword, on a storm-night I will tell you about when we reach the relics, and I put it back. If your song requires me to be a thief, find another taproom.

Excellent, traveler. Keep your mug close and your wits closer — the world of Hollowvale is full of objects that remember more than mortals ever could. Some are weapons, some are curses, and all have stories that make kings weep and heroes tremble.

# 📘 *The Grand Chronicle of Hollowvale*

## **Part V: Artifacts, Relics, and Legendary Weapons**

*As Told by the Keeper of the Embered Flagon*

---

### 🍻 *The Tavernkeeper Speaks*

Ah, artifacts… they say a sword is just a sword, a crown just a crown, but I’ve handled enough relics to know better. These objects carry whispers of the ages. They remember the fire, the blood, the courage, and the folly of those who wielded them. Some bring glory. Some bring ruin. And some… well, some are better left to gather dust in forgotten tombs.

Listen well, friend. I’ll tell you their stories.

---

### 🗡️ **The Sword of Aeltharion (Elarthyn)**

* **Origin**: Forged in the year 247 from a shard of fallen starlight, the star having struck the isle of Starfall in the age before the forging. Quenched in the heart of a dying dragon, in Stonehollow, under Seran Blackforge’s hand.
* **Abilities**: Slices through dragon-scale. Glows in the presence of corruption. Whispers in the ancient tongues of gods. A pure heart, the legends say, may bend a fate with it; a greedy hand corrupts the fate instead. I have seen the glow. I have not seen a fate do as it was told, and I distrust any weapon that offers the service.
* **Prophecy**: *“When the Last Flame rises, the Sword shall awaken, and ash will speak the truth of the world.”* The blade hums when the heart under the Cavern stirs. That hum has been mistaken for the awakening. It is a warning, not the deed.
* **The loss**: Lost in the year 500, in the collapse of Sky Pyre. Not recovered at the Sealing of 842. Not recovered by the expedition of 1402. Its whereabouts remain a mystery.
* **The glimpses**: Blue fire at Starfall Peak on the night of the fall — a memory in the rock, not a scabbard. A whisper in the Cavern’s halls on cold nights. Dreams in Brackenford, which are cheap. Ghost-light over Emberwood that belongs more honestly to Myravelle.
* **The splinter**: There is a shard broken from the tang, or from the star before the tang was finished — the **Star-Shard Splinter**. It hums the same note and cuts almost nothing. It is the thing a company can actually carry out of the **Cinder Vault** beside the Cavern mouth and not be lying when they say they touched the sword. It is not Elarthyn. Anyone who sells it to you as the sword is selling you a tuning fork and a funeral.
* **Future hook**: The true blade still waits. The main road I set under a newcomer’s feet ends, if they are brave and not greedy, at the splinter and the sealed stair above the heart. The recovery of Elarthyn itself is the deed beyond that stair, and it has not been done in any age I have poured through.
* **Historical Notes**: Lost during the fall of Sky Pyre; its whereabouts remain a mystery, though glimpses of blue fire in caverns and forests are whispered to be its wake.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“I once held it, for a night, after a storm. It hummed like a heartbeat in my chest. I returned it, of course — a blade like that does not belong to a man who pours ale for a living. I will not tell you the shelf I returned it to. I will tell you it was not the cellar, and it was not the Peak, and the person I returned it to is not alive in any way you can send a bill to.”*

---

### 🏹 **Ashstring, Lira’s Bow**

* **Origin**: The bow of Lira of the Emberwood. The last arrow loosed from it opened the seam in Veyrath’s scales in the year 500.
* **Where it went**: Vanished with her body. Her spirit became the first wraith; the bow did not become a ghost’s weapon in any sighting I trust. Hunters in the eastern and northern eaves sometimes find an unstrung stave of black yew that hums and then is not there. I think Ashstring is in the wood the way a regret is in a family. It is not a loot-drop for the quick-fingered. If it lets itself be carried, it will be because the carrier is about to die usefully.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“Do not follow a yew-hum into the trees after dark unless you have told someone where to bury you. She was kind, for a hero. Kindness in a wraith is a bowstring.”*

---

### ⛓️ **The Chains of the Shattered Flame**

* **Origin**: Forged by dwarves under Thurnok’s guidance — compelled guidance — and finished in the year 688, to bind dragons during the Second Age.
* **Properties**: Unbreakable by mortal hands, yet sensitive to the will of dragons. Chains can burn, melt, or constrict when activated by dragon magic.
* **Legends**: Some claim these chains can bind a god itself if reforged with blood, steel, and flame.
* **Not the same work**: The **Binding of Ember Peaks** (year 899) is a fastened prison in the mountain cores, reached by Chaincollar Pass. Same people, later century, different iron. Collecting scattered links will not unmake the Peaks, and breaking the Peaks will not hand you the scattered links.
* **Historical Notes**: Lost in scattered dwarven forges; rumors suggest pieces lie buried under Stonehold, under Emberwood, and in the Cavern of Embers. Thren Blackanvil reforged a gathered set once, in the late Second Age, and it was broken again on purpose.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“Handle with care. I’ve seen dwarves argue over a single link until the smith’s blood stained the anvil. Chains aren’t just metal, friend… they’re memory, anger, and history all bound tight.”*

---

### 👑 **The Cursed Crown of Vaelorin**

* **Origin**: Crafted from gold infused with the essence of Zhyranda the Gilded Maw’s last breath, during the Chaining Wars.
* **Properties**: Grants absolute dominion over mortals, but slowly corrupts the wearer’s mind.
* **Legends**: Kings who wore it became tyrants, sometimes overnight. Many were found dead with the crown’s filigree fused to their skulls. *“Beware the hand that lifts the crown of Vaelorin; the wearer’s soul shall melt as iron in flame.”*
* **Historical Notes**: Last seen during the fall of Vaelorin’s central citadel in 1423. The citadel is **Cindermark**, at the heart of the seven principalities. Its disappearance sparked wars over succession for decades, which is a gentle way to say the realm ended.
* **Where to look, if you are a fool**: The ruin of Cindermark, the hoard-vaults under Giltmere in Goldwake, and any Bone-Spear chief who has recently become eloquent. The crown teaches eloquence the way a forge teaches a fingertip.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“A fool tried to pawn it here once. Nearly turned the bar into a battlefield before I tossed it into the hearth. The hearth spat it back. I will not tell that story to the end while children are in the square.”*

---

### 📜 **The Grimoire of Whispered Flames**

* **Origin**: A lost tome containing incantations for manipulating ley-lines and communicating with dragons.
* **Properties**: Can grant mortal minds insight into dragonfire, elemental control, and ancient magics. Risk: permanent madness if read without preparation.
* **Legends**: Some claim it was written by Ardent the Magus, imbued with his soul before turning to ash.
* **Historical Notes**: Rumored to reside in **Elyndar ruins**. Those ruins have a name now: **Glimmerhollow**, deep in the Veiled Glades, two days under the Veil from its eastern edge. Fragments, not the whole book, have been sworn to by people who came out wrong. The whole book, if it is anywhere, is in the lower scriptorium of Glimmerhollow, behind a blue flame that incinerates the unworthy. The **Moonlit Grove**, a day east of Glimmerhollow, grants visions of the book’s pages and does not grant the book. Do not confuse a vision with a shelf.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“I’ve smelled the smoke of this book from a mile away. Many have tried to read it. None have returned sane. None.”*

---

### 🔮 **The Amulet of Seranya’s Tear**

* **Origin**: A single crystal formed from one of the goddess’ tears, collected when the first seas were born.
* **The name**: The goddess is **Serenya**. “Seranya” is a copyist’s vowel that stuck to the relic’s market-name. The crystal does not answer to the error. The peddlers do.
* **Properties**: Can heal, restore fertility, or amplify natural magic. Sometimes whispers warnings of looming disasters.
* **Legends**: Its bearer is said to be blessed, though jealousy follows close behind. Many queens, sorceresses, and shamans sought it, few ever found it.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“I keep a fragment in my cellar, though I dare not use it. Even a drop of divine water is heavier than a keg of ale in trouble. The fragment is real. The cellar is real. The right question gets you the stair, and a steady heart gets you back up it.”*

---

### ⚔️ **Other Notable Artifacts**

1. **The Embered Flagon Chalice** — Once used to seal dragon-blood pacts. Now rumored to grant prophetic visions if filled with molten metals. I held it. The next day the tavern was overrun with shadows. It is not currently behind the bar. If it is in the cellar, it is not in the same chest as the tear-fragment, because I learn slowly but I do learn.
2. **Nightfang Dagger** — Forged from the tooth of a dragon, perfect for silent kills or sorcery rituals. Night-Gleam chiefs pass a tooth among them and claim it is the one. One of those claims is true.
3. **Cloak of Elarion’s Shadow** — A cloak woven from dreamflame threads, allowing invisibility in moonlight. It wants the Veiled Glades. In full sun it is only a very good cloak, and annoyed about it.
4. **Stonehollow Hammer** — Used in the forging of the Chains; strikes carry the weight of dwarven wrath. Seran’s own hammer is a different tool and a greater one, and it has not been seen since he has not. The Stonehollow Hammer on the relic-lists is the Chainforge maul, and Thane Molle knows which locked niche is empty.

---

### 🧰 **The Gear of the Four Callings**

Not everything that matters is a legend. The company that drinks at the corner table of the Flagon, and the companies that replace them when the road keeps the receipt, go out in ordinary kit. I record it because a scholar with a relic-list and no sword is a meal.

* **The knight’s issue**: iron sword, wooden shield, chainmail, and a healing potion if Sella has been paid. Their drill is the old Vaelorin close-order, degraded into something a single body can use: a shield-bash to steal a heartbeat, a stance that spends footing to buy skin, a power strike that spends the shoulder.
* **The mage’s issue**: apprentice staff, spellbook, cloth robes, mana potions. The battle-charms actually taught on the Dike are firebolt, frost nova, and a thin arcane shield. The spellbook’s further leaves — lightning, a mana ward, blink, and the ugly one that drinks a life to fill yours — are in Part XI. Life-leech is how a mage starts walking toward the forbidden without noticing the door.
* **The thief’s issue**: twin daggers, leather, lockpicks, a poison vial. The picks matter in the Cinder Vault, where two of the chests are honest about being locked. Night-Gleam poison is stronger than the vial and less interested in your survival.
* **The dwarf’s issue**: battle axe, dwarf-forged armor, a pickaxe that is a tool until it is not, and an ale flask that does more for stamina than my honest ale will admit. They come from Stonehollow even when they will not say which hall.

Sella brews the healing potions from hearth-rite and bitterleaf. Mana potions, the real ones, want a drop from a ley-pool and a promise you may not intend to keep. Bramble Wick at the forge will reforge the iron and sometimes, if the steel remembers, tell you whose it was.

---

### 🌌 *Tavernkeeper’s Reflection*

Ah, yes. Each of these relics has seen kings rise and fall, armies crushed beneath dragon claws, and cults whispering in darkened catacombs. A mortal may hold them for a lifetime, but the objects themselves… they *remember*.

I once held the Embered Flagon Chalice. It hummed, as though alive. The next day, the tavern was overrun with shadows. Never again, I tell you. Some things are better left in legend.

But for those brave — or foolish — enough, these artifacts can shape the fate of Hollowvale itself. And believe me, friend, the world *will* remember them.

Very well, traveler. Pull up a chair, for the fireside tales grow long and the ale is nearly cold. This part is all about the players of Hollowvale — mortals, monsters, and madmen alike.

# 📘 *The Grand Chronicle of Hollowvale*

## **Part VI: Factions and Heroes**

*As Told by the Keeper of the Embered Flagon*

---

### 🍻 *The Tavernkeeper Speaks*

Ah, the mortals. Some born of courage, some of ambition, some of madness. And then there are the groups — the ones who shape history while the rest of us just pay taxes and spill ale. Listen well: factions matter, for they are the rivers through which power flows.

I will give you each faction as the rolls give them, and then I will tell you who, in the year 1477, still answers when the name is shouted.

---

### ⚔️ **The Order of the Shattered Flame**

* **Origins**: Founded in the First Age, year 365, as the war-banner of Seran, Lira, and Ardent. Shattered with them at Sky Pyre in the year 500. **Born in the Second Age**, year 640, as a college of keepers, by Dame Ossethe and Bramm Blackforge. Both sentences in the old rolls are true. They are two births.
* **Purpose**: Bind or slay dragons; protect dragon-binding knowledge; guard relics of flame.
* **Symbols**: A broken sword over a flickering flame.
* **Notable Heroes**:

  * *Seran Blackforge*: Founder and master smith. First birth. Gone since 500.
  * *Lira of the Emberwood*: Archer whose sacrifice opened the way for the Sword of Aeltharion to pierce Veyrath. First birth. First wraith.
  * *Ardent the Magus*: Elf whose magic first bound dragon essence. First birth. Ash.
  * *Dame Ossethe* and *Bramm Blackforge*: Second birth. Dust, honestly, and better dust than most.
* **Secrets**: Rumors say surviving members still meet in hidden halls, guiding events from the shadows. The rumors are about the second Order. In 1477 the name I trust for their Warden is **Calveth**. I have never been shown a hall on purpose. I have swept ash off my cellar step that was not my ash.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“I’ve seen their banners, faded and torn, yet I swear they glimmer when no one looks. If Calveth comes in, he drinks water, which is how I know he is serious and how I know he is not staying.”*

---

### 🕯️ **The Ashen Cult**

* **Origins**: Followers of Vareth the Pale, who preached from 1104 and disappeared into the ruins of Sky Pyre in 1130. The sect as an organization emerges in the Ashen Wastes in 1325.
* **Purpose**: Either redemption or apocalypse — interpretations vary among their cells. They want the Last Flame awakened. They mean the heart’s dream, and they are willing to mean the end of crops.
* **Symbols**: A blackened flame encircled by ash.
* **Notable Figures**:

  * *Vareth the Pale*: First prophet, disappeared into ruins of Sky Pyre. Not a present threat. A present scripture.
  * *High Seeress Ilthara*: Claimed to commune with the Last Flame through visions of fire and smoke. Holds the name in 1477, prolonged by the rites or replaced quietly. Her seat of work is the temple-shelf two days east of the Obsidian Monoliths, with cells in crypts and, unfortunately, in taverns that are not mine.
* **Activities**: Hidden rituals, infiltration of kingdoms, and attempts to recover legendary artifacts. The Sword is the tool their invocation wants. They do not have it. They have a great many people who have died proving that.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“Some of them have eyes like dying coals. Speak not to them unless you fancy a quick trip to the grave. Sulfur and old parchment. If a stranger smells of both, I have already counted the exits.”*

---

### 🐲 **The Goblin Tribes**

* **Origins**: Freed from dragon servitude after the First Age. Exodus by 547.
* **Purpose**: Survival, power, worship of flame, and sometimes chaos for its own sake.
* **Major Clans**:

  1. *Firefang*: Shamans of Emberwood, prophetic and cunning. Shrine in the west-deep. Warband in the Molten Halls.
  2. *Bone-Spear*: Raiders of the Ashen Wastes, collectors of relics. They run the ruined Ash Road in everything but law.
  3. *Night-Gleam*: Shadowy assassins rumored to commune with dragon spirits. Ruin-margins and the Veil’s edge. Poison is their coin.
* **Notable Leaders**:

  * *Grishnak Flameeye*: A cunning warlord who is, in 1477, the closest thing to a single banner the clans have had. Not crowned. Effective.
  * *Skrat Embertooth*: Holds the goblin fortifications in the Cavern for him.
  * *Vesska*: Shaman at Firefang Shrine. Will parley. Will also read your future in smoke and then charge you for the funeral.
* **How they fight, because you will meet them on the road**: The small quick ones with knives are the rank. The **archers** take the trees on the Ember Road and the ledges in the Halls. The **brutes** are the door-holders, heavier than they have any right to be, and they like a narrow place. All three are Firefang when you are in the wood or the Cavern, and the same shapes wear Bone-Spear colors in the ash. Do not negotiate with a brute. Negotiate with a shaman, from farther away than you think.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“They may be small, but one fire in a goblin heart can burn a village to ash before dawn. Grishnak’s clever, I’ll give him that. Clever goblins are still goblins — and blood tastes the same to them, no matter who spills it.”*

---

### ⛏️ **The Dwarves of Stonehollow**

* **Origins**: Forgers of the chains that bound dragons, deep miners of crystal and ore. Aeltharion’s breath, by their own catechism; Thurnok’s patrons, by their history; Thurnok’s jailers, by the part they do not carve over the gate.
* **Purpose**: Craft, survive, reclaim lost glory. Maintain the Binding. Never, if they can help it, let a prince of Vaelorin hold a complete chain.
* **The names of home**: Stonehollow the mountains, Stonehold the gate-city, Stonehold Deep the undercity. Stonevein Outpost is the southwestern station, half-abandoned since the Battle of Stonehollow in 1289.
* **Who rules in 1477**: **Thane Molle Deepmark**, at Stonehold. She is old enough to have been told the revolt by people who saw Durvak, and young enough to still check the collars herself. The Ironborn are a clan within the thanedom, thinned, proud, and legal. They are not a rebel state. The revolt was 1044.
* **Notable Figures**:

  * *Durvak Stonevein*: Leader of the Ironborn revolt, cursed with metal in his veins. Called **Durnak Ironborn** on human paper, and “Last of the Ironborn” by people who were not introduced. One dwarf.
  * *Thren Blackanvil*: Legendary smith who reforged the Chains of the Shattered Flame. Second Age. Dead. Quoted constantly.
* **Secrets**: They guard the location of remaining dragon remnants and powerful relics. Thurnok’s Prison is real, empty, and warm. Chaincollar Pass is patrolled and also collapsed in two places, which is the dwarven idea of a welcome.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“Dwarves drink like kings and fight like storms. Cross them at your peril. They remember every insult, every slight… and they never forget a debt. If one calls you Durnak by mistake, correct him gently. If a human calls Durvak ‘Durnak’ in front of one, step back.”*

---

### 👑 **Vaelorin and the Seven Princes**

* **What it was**: One kingdom from 588 to the fall of Cindermark in 1423.
* **What it is**: Seven principalities in a ring around a fallen citadel, all seeking remnants of dragon-blooded artifacts, none willing to let another wear a crown that may still be in the rubble.
* **Who rules in 1477**, and where they sit relative to Cindermark:

  1. **Hearthmarch**, to the west, seat **Ashford-on-Bracken**, thirty-two leagues west of the citadel and sixteen east of my door. Prince **Oswin Harth**. He charters Brackenford’s militia on paper and underpays it in practice. The closest prince. The one you will actually meet.
  2. **Stormbreak**, to the northwest, seat **Harrowgate**, twenty leagues from the citadel, two days’ ride from Stonevein Outpost when the thane allows the road. Princess **Maedh the Toll**. She taxes dwarven iron and calls it friendship.
  3. **Northmark**, to the north, seat **Frosthall**, eighteen leagues north of Cindermark, in the mountain-shadow. Prince **Calder Voss**. He pretends the Ice-Spine is his. The wyverns have not read the writ.
  4. **Redbanner**, to the east, seat **Sable Crossing**, eighteen leagues east. Prince **Roderic the Unspent**. Cavalry, parades, and a hunger for any relic that shows well behind a throne. Beyond him the grass goes on. We call it the **Unmarched East**. I have no distances for it. Anyone who does is selling a conquest.
  5. **Goldwake**, to the southeast, seat **Giltmere**, sixteen leagues from the citadel. Princess **Ilyra Quench**. Richest, nearest to Zhyranda’s old hunger, and the reason the crown-rumors have money behind them.
  6. **Mourngate**, to the south, seat of the same name, sixteen leagues south of Cindermark, two days on the Dike. Prince **Serran Ash**. He faces the Wastes. His name is a bard’s joke on Seran, and he has killed men for less. The Ash Road once left his gate for the monoliths. It has not been a road since 1350.
  7. **Veilward**, to the southwest, seat **Larkspire**, fourteen leagues from the citadel. Prince **Elowyn Dast**. He faces the long ruined way toward the Glades and pretends the elves remember his title. They remember his tax collectors. There is a difference.

* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“Seven princes and not a king among them. If you must take a side, take Hearthmarch’s coin and do not wear their livery into Goldwake. Ilyra collects interesting people, and she does not return them in the same temper.”*

---

### 🔥 **The Free Tribes of Gorn**

* **Who**: Nomadic humans of the south, wild, often raiders, worshipping fire as freedom itself. They are not a goblin clan and they are not a principality. Princes call them bandits when the princes are losing livestock, and allies when a cousin needs a knife in Mourngate.
* **Where**: The seasonal **Moonshadow Camp**, on the ground the Ashfall scoured in 1350, two days southeast of the Veiled Glades’ eastern edge and three days northwest of the Obsidian Monoliths. Raised each autumn. Hidden the rest of the year in circuits only they can walk.
* **Who speaks in 1477**: **Mother-of-Ashes Ruka**. She decides whether the camp stands and whether you may see it. She is not a queen. Argue the point and you will be educated by the weather.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“They drink standing up, which is the only theology of theirs I have confirmed. Fire is freedom, they say. Freedom, I have noticed, still takes your horse.”*

---

### 🧝 **The Elyndar Remnants**

* **Who**: Elves hidden in the Veiled Glades, protectors of the ley-lines and old magics. More ghost than flesh since the Veil dropped in 940.
* **Where**: The Glades, southwest of Emberwood. Their city-ruin is **Glimmerhollow**. Their vision-grove is the **Moonlit Grove**.
* **Who speaks in 1477**: **Speaker Ithrael**. “Rules” is the wrong word and they will let you know, at length, without raising a voice. Ithrael permits, refuses, and occasionally walks a stranger back to the marches with all his memories of the path gently wrong.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“The elves weave spells like a spider weaves webs — beautiful, intricate… and deadly if you stumble into them. Bring no chain-link into Glimmerhollow. They can smell the compulsion on the iron, and Thurnok was not their friend.”*

---

### 🏕️ **The Dike-folk, called Halflings**

The old bestiaries mention halflings only as raiders, which is like mentioning humans only as princes. The **Dike-folk** keep the waystations on the King’s Dike, farm the small plots between Ashford and the citadel-road, and produce, yes, a steady minority of bandits in the ruins. They are a people, not a faction with a throne. Their largest cluster of roofs is **Lowford**, a waystation ten leagues east of Brackenford, six leagues short of Ashford. If you are robbed politely and the thief apologizes to your horse, it was probably them. If you are robbed and set on fire, it was not.

---

### 🌟 **Heroes of the Third Age**

* **Kaelen the Ash-Walker**: Cursed knight wandering ruins, searching for the Sword of Aeltharion. Still the searching kind of dead-alive. Seen on the Ash Road and once, may it not repeat, at my cellar turn.
* **Serra of the Embered Veil**: Human sorceress who spoke to the dragon’s heart and shaped wars with the answers. Her grave is unmarked on purpose, somewhere in Veilward. The prophecies outlived her, which was her intention and everyone else’s problem.
* **Durvak Stonevein**, called Durnak Ironborn: One dwarf. Revolt in 1044. Wandered after. Rumored still in the Deep.
* **The Cursed Knight**: A wraith born of the First Age’s sword-curse, not Lira herself. He was a knight of the *second* Order who swore himself to Elarthyn, died unreleased, and hunts those who bear legendary weapons. “Born from the First Age” in the old roll means born of the curse that began when the sword tasted divine blood, not born in the year 500 as a man. Lira is the first wraith, the Ashstring shade in the eaves. The Knight rides in black armor and does not carry a bow. If your ballad merges them, your ballad will get someone embraced by the wrong ghost.

---

### 🧭 *What They Eat, Bury, and Refuse*

A short telling, because culture is not a costume. It is what a people will not do even when you pay them.

* **Brackenford humans** rebuild. Their law is the militia-captain’s shout and my right to refuse service. They bury in the ridge east of town, feet toward the river, and they toast the dead with the deceased’s own mug, which is unsanitary and correct. They fear the wood and live on its edge anyway.
* **Vaelorin humans** inherit. Law is the prince’s seal. They bury with a coin for the citadel that no longer collects it. They will start a war over a genealogy and call it piety.
* **Dwarves** ledger. A debt is a moral object. They bury in the stone of the hall the dead earned, not in a field. They will share ale and not share a forge-secret, and they consider the second more intimate.
* **Elyndar** remember. They do not bury so much as *consent to become story*. A name said wrongly three times is a kind of death to them. They will heal a stranger and mislay his way home.
* **Gorn** move. Ownership is what you can carry and defend at evening. They burn their dead, which the hearth-cult finds respectful and the princes find evidence.
* **Goblins** negotiate with fire. Oaths are smoke-oaths, released when the fire dies, which is why you want the wording tight and the fuel wet. They eat what the country gives, including, if the country is the Cavern, things that are still glowing.
* **The cult** does not bury if it can burn, and it writes the name in the ash. This is not a culture you are invited to join. It is a culture that joins you.

---

### 🧩 *Tavernkeeper’s Reflection*

You see, friend, power is never lonely. Factions are webs, heroes are threads, and the world itself is a loom. Every tavern, crypt, and battlefield carries the imprint of these players.

And me? I pour the ale, tell the tales, and watch the pieces move. Some day, perhaps, you’ll see these stories come alive… and some day, perhaps, you’ll choose which side you stand on.

Choose before you are chosen. The road does it for you if you dawdle, and the road has poor judgment and excellent timing.

*Next: Part VII — “Regions of Hollowvale” — an atlas of lands, villages, forests, mountains, and caverns, with the days of walking written where the old copies only shrugged.*

---
Ah, excellent. Pull up a stool, traveler, for now we’ll chart the lands themselves. Every hill, forest, and river has a tale, and the world of Hollowvale is full of secrets that only the brave—or foolish—dare uncover. The old tellings gave you the names and trusted you to get lost. I have counted the roads out on this bar with mugs and regret. You will have bearings.

# 📘 *The Grand Chronicle of Hollowvale*

## **Part VII: Regions of Hollowvale**

*As Told by the Keeper of the Embered Flagon*

---

### 🍻 *The Tavernkeeper Speaks*

Ah, the lands… some call them maps, others call them cursed. But every village, every forest, every cavern carries the footprints of dragons, heroes, and the damned. Know them well, for a step wrong in Hollowvale can be your last.

---

### 📏 *The Measure*

I will use one measure, and I will not change it when the song would prefer a vaguer one.

* A **league** is the distance a laden traveler walks in an hour on a fair road.
* A **day on foot** is eight leagues on a road, six in forest, five in the ash, and four in true mountain.
* A **day on a horse** is sixteen leagues on a road, ten in open forest-edge, and a courtesy in the dunes and the ice — you will walk the beast or you will bury it.
* The dragon’s death-scream “heard for a hundred leagues” uses this same league. It crossed the known mainland. It did not cross the sea to the Isles.

When I say north I mean the Ice-Spine’s direction. West is the sea. Brackenford is the peg I hang the other distances on, because you are sitting in it, and because I am not getting up.

---

### 🗺️ *The Lay of the Land*

Hold the shape before the details.

The **Western Sea** is the left hand of the world, Serenya’s tears. Its far water, twelve days’ sail out from the Graycoast, boils. That is the **Boiling Reach**, and the **Forgotten Isles** stand in it.

The mainland’s western shore is the **Graycoast**, cliff and cove, running from the Ice-Spine’s sea-meeting in the north down to the elven water in the south. **Saltmercy** is the last honest harbor. **Cindercove**, four days’ coast-walk south of Saltmercy, is where the Veilwater finds the sea.

Inland of the Graycoast, after the Coast Track, stands **Emberwood**, the great central wood. It runs about thirty-six leagues east to west — six days if you walk the timber itself — and about twenty-four leagues north to south — four days in the trees. **Brackenford** sits on its easternmost eaves, where the trees give onto grass.

North of the wood the land rises into foothills, and then into the **Northern Peaks**, an east-west wall. The western and central high country of that wall is the **Ice-Spine**, wyvern ground. The northeastern mass, where the stone turns to iron and obsidian, is **Stonehollow**.

East of Brackenford the **Bracken Plains** run to Vaelorin. The fallen citadel **Cindermark** is forty-eight leagues east of my door. Beyond Redbanner, east again, I stop drawing. That is the Unmarched East.

South and southeast of the wood, sharing the hem where Veilroot burned, lie the **Ashen Wastes**. They touch Emberwood. They are not a rumor on the far side of a kinder country. From Fallowridge’s ridge you can see the first grey.

Southwest of the wood, past the wet **Willowmarches**, lie the **Veiled Glades**.

The **main quest triangle**, since you will be sent along it before you are sent anywhere wiser, is this: the **Embered Flagon** in Brackenford, on the eastern edge of Emberwood; the **Ember Road**, called in the old notes the Road to the Cavern; and the **Cavern of Embers**, with the **Sky Pyre** ruins on the ridge two leagues north of its mouth. The Cavern is northwest of my door, north of the wood, in the foothills. The road does not go there in a straight line, because a straight line is how goblins eat.

---

### 🌊 *Seas, Coasts, and Harbors*

* **The Western Sea** — The great water west of the Graycoast. Serenya’s tears, in the myth, and cold ordinary salt once you are a day off the boiling. No eastern sea is known to me. Do not let a prince sell you one.
* **The Boiling Reach** — The western deep, where the sea heats around the Forgotten Isles. Twelve days’ sail due west from Saltmercy. The water smokes. Hulls tarred for honest oceans come apart.
* **The Graycoast** — The mainland’s western shore, from the northern sea-cliffs under the Ice-Spine down to Cindercove. Mist in the morning even when the Reach is days away. Fire-licked cliffs begin only as you close on the isles, not here.
* **Saltmercy** — Last harbor on the Graycoast willing to speak of the Isles out loud. Four days west of Emberwood’s western eaves by the Coast Track. From Brackenford that is six days through the timber and then those four, or longer if you go around the wood on the southern eaves and up the coast. Captain Pell Seaburn drinks here.
* **Cindercove** — Four days’ coast-walk south of Saltmercy. Mouth of the Veilwater. Elf-boats, when they exist, are mistaken for fog.

---

### 🏞️ *Rivers and Lakes*

Aeltharion wove these. Dragons kinked them. I have walked five of the six.

* **The Bracken River** — Rises in the Ley-Line Pools of southern Emberwood, flows east out of the trees, through the ford that names Brackenford, and on east across the plains to Ashford. Drinkable if you let the mineral settle. Glows faintly on a windless night for a mile below the pools, and then pretends to be ordinary. From the pools to my ford is a day and a half if you follow the bank, ten leagues by the water’s own bend, shorter through the trees if you know the cut.
* **The Anvilmelt** — Comes south out of Stonehollow, snowmelt warmed by the forges, and bends southwest to meet the Bracken at Ashford. Dwarves say it is their river and humans fish it anyway. A horse does the mountain-foot to Ashford in three days; I would not.
* **The Cinderflow** — The joined water below Ashford. Runs south through Mourngate and dies in the **Salt-Ash Sink**, a bitter steaming lake in the northeastern wastes, a day’s walk west of Mourngate’s outer wall. Fish in the Sink are a dare, not a meal.
* **The Pyre Run** — A short, hot stream born at the Cavern’s lower vents. Flows south into the northern eaves of Emberwood and sinks into the ground before it can reach the Ley-Line Pools. Not drinkable. Not a road. Goblins wash steel in it and call that tempering.
* **The Veilwater** — Rises under Glimmerhollow, flows west through the Glades, and reaches the sea at Cindercove. Clear, cold, and likely to show you a face that is not yours if you kneel.
* **The Graymere** — Comes out of **Lake Merrow** in the Ice-Spine, flows southwest along the west side of Emberwood, and meets the sea a day’s coast-walk north of Saltmercy. This is the western river. It is not the Bracken, and the number of maps that marry them is a scandal.
* **Lake Merrow** — A black-cold tarn high in the Ice-Spine, four days’ climb north of the western mouth of Wyrmgap. Source of the Graymere. Wyverns drink there in pairs and do not share.
* **The Mere of Cinders** — The small steaming lake at the foot of Blackspire Keep, six leagues east of Fallowridge. Myravelle’s ashfall pooled here. The keep stands on the basalt spike above it.
* **The Ley-Line Pools** — Not a gentle lake. A basin of bright water in southern Emberwood, ten leagues southwest of Brackenford through the trees, a day and a half. They are the head of the Bracken and a convergence of ley-lines. Mages use them. The wood uses the mages who stay too long.
* **The Salt-Ash Sink** — Already named. End of the Cinderflow. Steams. Lies in the northeastern Ashen Wastes.

---

### 🛤️ *Roads*

* **The Ember Road**, which the quest-rolls call the **Road to the Cavern**. Leaves Brackenford by the **south gate** — the lower gate, the one every traveler takes, because the village faces its danger downhill and lets the road find north on its own. It runs **four leagues due south** to **Hearthmile Stone**, half a day on foot. There it bends **west-northwest** and follows the northern eaves. **Sixteen leagues** of that bend bring you to **Ruinwatch**, the camp opposite the Ashen Ruins, two days on foot from the stone, one day in the saddle. Goblin ambushes belong to this leg: archers in the twisted trees, brutes where the track pinches. From Ruinwatch the road turns **north**, **eight leagues** into the foothills, one day climbing, to the **Cavern mouth**. A horse wants that last day on its own feet as much as you do. **Total: twenty-eight leagues, three and a half days on foot, two days on a horse.** The playable first stretch, the wooded path a newcomer walks with the village at their back, is the south-gate leg and the start of the bend. The Cavern is not south of the village. The first hour is.
* **The King’s Dike** — The old royal road east. Raised against Bracken floods. Brackenford to **Lowford** ten leagues, to **Ashford** sixteen leagues (two days on foot, one on a horse), to **Cindermark** forty-eight leagues all told (six days on foot, three on a horse). Princely seats hang off it like bad jewelry.
* **The Ash Road** — The ruined caravan route. It once ran from Mourngate southwest to Blackspire’s country, with a spur to Fallowridge, then on to the Obsidian Monoliths, northwest to Moonshadow, and on toward the Glade-veil. Broken in the year 1350. What remains is a line of half-buried posts and a habit of ghosts. Walking the broken sections is five leagues a day when the ash is polite.
* **The Coast Track** — From Emberwood’s western eaves west to Saltmercy, four days, thirty-two leagues of scrub and salt wind.
* **The Deeping Stair** — Not a public road. The dwarven way from Stonevein Outpost north to the Stonehold gate, sixteen leagues, two days’ climb. The last day is steps.

---

### ⛰️ *Passes*

* **Wyrmgap** — The pass between the Ice-Spine on the west and Stonehollow on the east, in the northern wall. Three days’ climb north from the Sky Pyre ridge. Ice-Spine Wyverns hunt the western walls of the gap, in pairs. Dwarf patrols, rare and unfriendly, walk the eastern walls. This is the way to the high north faces, and the way a fool freezes in sight of both peoples’ warnings.
* **Chaincollar Pass** — The pass under the Ember Peaks where the year-899 chains enter the mountain cores. Three days’ climb above the Stonehold gate. Guarded, collapsed in two pitches, and hotter than a pass has any business being. Thane Molle’s writ or a very fast apology.

---

### 🌿 *Biomes*

The land wears three coats that a wanderer can name without a scholar, and you will meet all three on the trails that leave the Cavern mouth. I did not invent the coats to make the walk interesting. The country did.

* **Emberwood forest** — The deep wood. Twisted trees, green so dark it drinks torchlight, glowing fungi, rock, thorn-bush, and a path that exists only where feet have argued with roots. Dense. Humming on windless nights. This is the timber itself, not the grass at its edge.
* **The plains** — Open grass. The Bracken Plains east of the wood, the drier skirts of the Willowmarches, the verges of the King’s Dike, and the short turf of the Ashfall Flats before the grey takes over. Bush and stone, few trees, a long horizon. Brackenford’s fields are this coat. So is the first hour south of the gate, before the bend enters the eaves.
* **The ashen wastes** — Grey sand, ash crust, rock, and little else that grows on purpose. Storms that sand the skin off a story. The true wastes, south and southeast of the wood, and the old Veilroot tongue that runs along the southern hem. Fingers of this coat blow up the foothills and touch the trails southeast of the Cavern mouth, which is why a walker who leaves the mouth can pass from grass to greenwood to ash in a day’s wandering without a prince’s permission.
* **The Willowmarches** — Wet plains, two days to cross at their wide middle, standing between Emberwood’s southwestern eaves and the Veiled Glades. Not a fourth world. A plains-coat with more water and worse humor.
* **Ice-Spine and Stonehollow** — Mountain. Ice and granite to the west and center of the northern wall; iron, obsidian, and forge-heat to the northeast. Not the wastes, though both are unkind. Four leagues is a proud mountain-day.
* **The Boiling Reach** — Sea, and then not sea. Mist, heated water, fire-licked cliff. The biome of the Isles is rock, roost, and sealed door, with a shore that hisses.

The meeting-place has a name. The **Three-Country Trail** leaves the Cavern mouth on its southeastern slope — the dirt track at the lower side of the entrance, if you are standing in the mouth looking out. East and south of it, grass. West, the deep eaves of Emberwood. Southeast, ash-fingers and, if you are stubborn for days, the Wastes proper. Wilderness, the rangers call it, and they are not being poetic.

---

### 🏘️ **Brackenford Village**

* **Description**: A bustling human settlement on the edge of Emberwood Forest. Cobblestone streets, thatched roofs, and the ever-smoky tavern *The Embered Flagon*.
* **Where it stands**: On the **eastern eaves** of Emberwood, at the ford where the Bracken River comes out of the trees and turns east across the plains. The wood is immediately west. The Bracken Plains open immediately east. Fallowridge is **eight leagues due south**, one day on foot. Ashford is **sixteen leagues due east** by the King’s Dike. The Cavern of Embers is **twenty-eight leagues** by the Ember Road, northwest once the road has finished its southward dogleg — three and a half days on foot, two on a horse. Founded 1213. Living, loud, and smaller than its stories.
* **The shape of the place, so you can walk it**: The **Embered Flagon** stands at the heart, its door facing the square; the sign is a mug, and the door is under it. The **market** is on the west side of the square, toward the trees — Hest’s stalls, upper west, a short walk from the inn and not a journey. The **south gate** is the lower gate, bottom of the village as you face the road, and it puts you on the Ember Road. The smithy sits off the square toward the river. Storyteller’s Square is the open cobble between inn and market, whatever the planners wanted it to be.
* **Notable Features**:

  * *The Embered Flagon Tavern*: Heart of rumors, quest hooks, and occasional brawls. The Keeper — I — tend the bar at the room’s center. The cellar is a quest of its own and is not on the public stair.
  * *Blacksmith’s Forge*: Bramble Wick reforges weapons and occasionally whispers secrets of lost relics. He is not a dwarf. He is a human who has listened to dwarves more than is good for a human.
  * *Storyteller’s Square*: Where prophecy and legend are recited. Old Pate holds the midday hour and will invent a distance if you let him. Prefer mine.
  * *The market*: Hest Mallow sells kit, gossip, and apples. She is the vendor you deal with, not a wandering abstraction.
  * *Sella’s niche*: The Ford-Witch keeps Serenya’s shrine-stone by the water and brews the healing potions. She is not a cultist. She will tell you if you are about to become one.
* **Who keeps order**: Captain **Yselle Dorn** of the militia, a veteran of the goblin raids and of one very educational visit to the northern eaves. Her writ is the palisade and the first leg of the Ember Road, out to Hearthmile Stone. Past the stone you are a story she may avenge and will not accompany.
* **Local Lore**: Villagers whisper of goblin scouts in Emberwood and of the Sword of Aeltharion being glimpsed in dreams. The scouts are real. The dreams are mostly the mushrooms.
* **What walks the immediate country**: Goblin scouts in the eaves, plains-wolves, the occasional cinderboar in the fields, Dike-folk on the east road, and adventurers, who have buried more neighbors than the wolves have.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“Brackenford is small, but it’s seen more adventurers pass through than some kingdoms see in centuries. Keep your purse close and your wits closer. If you leave by the south gate you are already on the quest, whether you accepted it or not.”*

---

### 🌲 **Emberwood Forest**

* **Description**: Dense woods with twisted trees and glowing fungi. Home to goblin tribes and ruins of dragon shrines. The emberwood-forest coat: dark green, root, rock, bush, and fungus-light.
* **Where it stands**: The great wood west of Brackenford. Eastern eaves at the village. Western eaves thirty-six leagues west, six days through the trees, and from those eaves four days more to Saltmercy. Northern eaves under the foothills and the Ember Road. Southern and southeastern eaves shared with the Ashen Wastes along the old burn of Veilroot. The Willowmarches wet its southwestern corner, two days across at their middle, and beyond them the Veiled Glades.
* **Notable Sites**:

  * *Firefang Shrine*: Worship site of goblin shamans, imbued with lingering dragon magic. It was Vaelra’s grove-stones before it was theirs. It stands in the **western deep**, twenty-two leagues west of Brackenford through the timber, **four days** if the Firefang let you walk. Vesska is the shaman who matters. From the shrine to the Ley-Line Pools is fourteen leagues southeast, two days in the trees.
  * *Ashen Ruins*: Remnants of an ancient dragon fortress, partially collapsed into the forest floor. This was **Korvhal’s** ground-hold, not Sky Pyre. It lies in the **northeastern quarter** of the wood, **three leagues — half a day’s walk — due south of Ruinwatch** on the Ember Road. Ambushes thicken as the road comes abreast of it. A day and a half west-northwest of Brackenford if you cut the trees instead of taking the road, which is how people become lessons.
  * *Ley-Line Pools*: Magical pools that amplify elemental energy, often used by rogue mages. **Ten leagues southwest** of Brackenford, a day and a half through the trees, in a low basin. Headwater of the Bracken. Humming. On a windless night the hum is the one I promised you in the first hour of this chronicle.
  * *Ruinwatch*: Not a village. A ring of stones and a bad habit of camping, on the Ember Road, sixteen leagues west-northwest of Hearthmile Stone. Named because you can see Korvhal’s broken towers from it. People camp here. Goblins tax the decision.
* **Hazards**: Ambushes by goblins — scouts, archers, brutes — cursed beasts, enchanted traps, and the Ashstring shade if you are the sort of unlucky that ballads like.
* **What walks here**: Firefang of all three war-shapes, Firefang Drakes in the west-deep, Shadowfang Wolves, Ley-Line Serpents by the pools, cinderboars on the eastern edge, and Myravelle’s ghost over the canopy when the wind wants an audience.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“Many who enter Emberwood seeking glory never return. The forest remembers. Stay on the Ember Road until you have a reason and a shaman’s name. ‘I wanted to see the fungi’ is not a reason. It is a eulogy.”*

---

### 🏔️ **Cavern of Embers**

* **Description**: Molten rivers and glowing stalactites, created from Veyrath the First Flame’s dying body. A dragon’s heart still burns deep below. He is dead. The country is his corpse. The rumor that he survives is the rumor that the heart dreams. I have said this and I will keep saying it until the songs tire.
* **Where it stands**: In the foothills where the Northern Peaks begin, **north of Emberwood’s northern eaves**, **northwest of Brackenford** by the road’s true bearing. Eight leagues north of Ruinwatch. Twenty-eight leagues from my south gate by the Ember Road. The Three-Country Trail leaves the mouth’s southeastern slope into grass, greenwood, and ash-fingers. East along the foothills, Stonevein Outpost is eight days on foot. The Ice-Spine rises beyond Sky Pyre; Stonehollow is the wall farther east, not the wall directly above the mouth.
* **Sky Pyre ruins**: On the ridge **two leagues due north** of the mouth, half a day’s scramble, no horse-trail. The cloud-fortress fell here because its lord fell here. Some stones still hang a handspan off the ground. Vareth went in. The Order’s hidden hall is cut somewhere in that fallen stone, and I would not knock.
* **Notable Features**:

  * *The mouth*: The dungeon-gate of the country. You come up from the road into a rock jaw. The way back to the path is the lower side of the entrance. The way deeper is inward and up into the dark of the jaw, which then, unkindly, goes down.
  * *Molten Halls*: The first deep. Navigate carefully; one misstep and the earth swallows you. This is the goblin-held interior a company can actually fight through: bridges of cooled slag, a river of his blood to one side, and ledges for archers.
  * *Goblin Fortifications*: Warbands guarding treasure and secrets, often under mysterious enchantments. Skrat Embertooth holds them for Grishnak. Expect the rank and file, the brutes at the doors, and the archers above.
  * *The Cinder Vault*: A side-gallery off the mouth, not the heart and not the Halls. The secret door is in the upper face of the entrance, easy to miss if you only stare at the descent. Chests, some of them honestly locked. This is where the Star-Shard Splinter has been sworn to, and where lesser dragon-loot goes to be argued over. It is the treasure room of the Cavern, not the throne room.
  * *Dragon’s Heart Chamber*: Below a sealed stair at the bottom of the Halls, a day’s descent from the mouth if the stair were open. It is not open. The last ember of Veyrath — the Last Flame, properly named — burns there under the chains of the year 842. An **Ember Drake** nests against the sealed stair, fed by the Firefang as a pilgrimage with teeth. Killing the drake is the dragon-fight the road can offer you. Opening the stair is a different life.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“Step carefully here. The earth still remembers the claw of a god, and some rocks bite back. If you came for the sword, you came early by one stair and several centuries of other people’s deaths.”*

---

### 🏝️ **The Forgotten Isles**

* **Description**: Far western isles shrouded in mist, said to contain sleeping dragons and ancient sealed gates.
* **Where they stand**: In the Boiling Reach of the Western Sea, **twelve days’ sail due west of Saltmercy**. Three principal isles, stacked **north to south**, a day’s sail between neighbors. The mainland’s western sea between Graycoast and the Reach is cold salt; the boiling and the fire-licked cliffs belong to the last two days of the crossing. Maps were scarce in the Third Age and are not much fatter now. Pell’s chart is the one I trust, and it is stained with something that is not ink.
* **The three isles**:

  * *Starfall Isle*, the northernmost. **Starfall Peak** is the summit at its center: where the star that became the sword first struck, where the sword was glimpsed the night Sky Pyre fell, and where the 1402 expedition decided it was an ancient dragon observation post. Half a day’s climb from the only cove Pell will name, on the isle’s eastern lee.
  * *Gate Isle*, the middle isle, a day’s sail south of Starfall. **The Gates of Flame** stand on the eastern cliffs, facing the mainland as if the mainland had earned it. Magical portals sealed by sorcery older than Sky Pyre, possibly the lock on the rift from the Age Before Time, possibly a dragon-door. Both can be true if the gods were economical. They are not open. An expedition that tries them is a cooking-pot with ambitions.
  * *Catacomb Isle*, the southern, half-sunk, a day’s sail south of Gate Isle. **The Sunken Catacombs** run along its drowned eastern shore: ruins of long-lost civilizations, traps, relics, and water that is warm from below. You wade to the first gate and you swim, badly, to the second.
* **Legends**: Sailors claim the seas boil around the isles when the Last Flame stirs. The seas boil regardless, but they boil *worse* then, and the difference has convinced men who do not impress easily.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“Few live to tell of the Isles. Those that do… don’t believe what they saw, and neither will you. If you see fire in the mist, don’t assume it’s a lighthouse. Saltmercy will still be twelve days east of your regret.”*

---

### 🌵 **The Ashen Wastes**

* **Description**: A cursed desert, grey sands, ash storms, and relics of empires burned to the ground. The ashen-wastes coat: grey-brown crust, rock, no kindly trees.
* **Where they stand**: **South and southeast of Emberwood, sharing the border.** The northern lip is the **Ashfall Flats**, two leagues south of Fallowridge’s square — two hours’ walk from the rebuilt square, long enough for the tea to become a memory and the regret to become a plan. From that lip the wastes run about eight days’ march north to south at a survivor’s pace, and about ten days east to west at the widest. The western tongue is burned Veilroot, running along the wood’s southern hem until the Willowmarches. The eastern reach climbs toward Mourngate and the Salt-Ash Sink. The 1350 blast did not fly across a friendly kingdom. It rolled north across this border and took the roofs that were standing on it.
* **Notable Sites**:

  * *Obsidian Monoliths*: Strange black towers rising from ash, some say they are the anchors of ley-lines. They stand in the **central wastes**, **four days south of Fallowridge**, about twenty leagues of ash. A ring, not a city. Bone-Spear do not camp inside the ring after dark, which is the best recommendation I can give you.
  * *Ashen Cult Temples*: Hidden structures where cultists attempt to awaken the Last Flame. Dug into a basalt shelf **two days east of the Monoliths**. Not marked on the caravan posts. Ilthara’s working-seat is the deepest of them. If you find it by accident you have been invited, and the invitation is the danger.
  * *Ruined Caravan Routes*: The Ash Road, as broken in 1350. Haunted by spirits, cursed creatures, and the **Phantom Caravan**, which the side-tales say appears once a century. It appeared in 1400-and-a-bit and is not due. Do not wait for it as a plan.
  * *The Salt-Ash Sink*: Northeastern wastes, a day west of Mourngate. End of the Cinderflow.
* **What walks here**: Bone-Spear raiders, Shadowfang Wolves, Ashen Revenants in the storms, the Ash Wyrm if the ley is bleeding, cultists, and ash-lurkers under the crust.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“Cross the Wastes lightly. The wind carries whispers, but the sand buries more than it reveals. Take the Ash Road’s posts while they last, and when they stop, stop with them.”*

---

### ⛰️ **Stonehollow, Stonehold, and the Ember Peaks**

* **Where they stand**: The **northeastern** reach of the northern mountain wall. From the Cavern mouth, east along the foothills, **eight days on foot** — near fifty leagues of bad track — to **Stonevein Outpost**. From Brackenford, northeast across the plains and up, **fifty leagues, eight days on foot, five on a horse**, to the same outpost. The Ice-Spine is the western neighbor of this range, with Wyrmgap between them. Vaelorin’s Stormbreak and Northmark sit in the shadow to the south.
* **Stonevein Outpost** — Dwarven mining station on the **southwestern flank** of Stonehollow, the last roof before the wild foothills. **Sixteen leagues south** of the Stonehold gate by the Deeping Stair, two days’ climb. Partially abandoned after the dragon skirmish of the year 1289, the Battle of Stonehollow. Not empty. A watch, a winch, and more grudge than garrison. The Stonevein name is the clan’s; Durvak’s line is from here in the way a storm is from a valley.
* **Stonehold** — The gate-city on the south face, humans’ shortening of the hold of Stonehollow. Thane **Molle Deepmark** rules here in 1477. Forges, the Chainforge niche, and the upper end of the Deeping Stair.
* **Stonehold Deep** — The undercity beneath the gate. Third Age delving: obsidian veins, angry crystal, the **Ironborn Gallery**, the **Chainforge**, and **Thurnok’s Prison**, which is empty and warm. Days of descent. Not a place you visit because the thane likes your axe. A place you visit because you are returning a link or answering a debt.
* **The Ember Peaks** — A knot of peaks **within** Stonehollow, not a separate range and not the Ice-Spine. The Binding of the year 899 is in their cores. **Three days’ climb above Stonehold** by Chaincollar Pass. Hot stone, cold wind, chains going into the mountain like stitches. Dwarves call the knot the Ember Peaks. Humans sometimes say “the Stonehollow peaks” and are close enough to be annoying rather than lost.
* **The Battle’s scar**: In 1289 rogue dragons — lesser brood, not the Seven — came for the Chains. The dwarves repelled them. A stretch of the deep roads collapsed and is still unwalked. Stonevein lost half its people and most of its cheer.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“If you owe them, go. If you are curious, write a letter. Curiosity in a mine is just a cave-in that can talk.”*

---

### 🏰 **Vaelorin**

* **Where it stands**: East of the Bracken Plains. The fallen heart, **Cindermark**, is **forty-eight leagues east of Brackenford** by the King’s Dike, six days on foot, three on a horse, through Hearthmarch. The realm is the ring of seven principalities around that ruin. North of it, Stonehollow’s shadow. South of it, Mourngate and then the wastes. West of it, eventually, me.
* **Cindermark** — The central citadel, fallen in 1423. Gold-sweated stone, a hole where a dynasty was. The Cursed Crown was last seen in that fall. The ruin sits in a bowl the seven princes can all see and none of them hold. A day-camp of rival banners is the usual population. Treat every sentry as the true one.
* **The seven**, with bearings from the citadel, repeated here so the atlas has them in the ground and not only in the faction-roll:

  * Ashford-on-Bracken, Hearthmarch, thirty-two leagues west.
  * Harrowgate, Stormbreak, twenty leagues northwest.
  * Frosthall, Northmark, eighteen leagues north.
  * Sable Crossing, Redbanner, eighteen leagues east. Past it, the Unmarched East, which I am not mapping.
  * Giltmere, Goldwake, sixteen leagues southeast.
  * Mourngate, sixteen leagues south, the gate toward the wastes.
  * Larkspire, Veilward, fourteen leagues southwest.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“United, they crowned a dragon’s breath and called it law. Fractured, they do the same thing with less architecture. Do your business in Ashford. Count your fingers afterward anyway.”*

---

### 🌙 **The Veiled Glades**

* **Where they stand**: **Southwest of Emberwood.** From Brackenford, along the southern eaves, **four days on foot**, twenty-four leagues, to the eastern edge of the Veil. That walk crosses the northern skirt of the Willowmarches; the marches’ wide middle is two days of wet going on its own. The Glades are what the elves saved of the old wood when Veilroot burned. They are moon-forest: pale trunks, heavy canopy, paths that agree with you only while you are welcome.
* **Glimmerhollow** — Elven ruins deep in the Glades, **two days west** of the veil-edge under the canopy. Rumored to hold fragments of the Grimoire of Whispered Flames, and possibly the book. Speaker Ithrael’s people keep the lower scriptorium the way a body keeps a heart, by not letting strangers poke it. The Veilwater rises here.
* **The Moonlit Grove** — A grove, not the city. **One day east of Glimmerhollow**, still under the canopy, one day in from the veil-edge if you come by the southern approach. Under the full moon it grants visions, or curses, depending on whether you came to look or to take. It is not Glimmerhollow with a prettier name. Walk a day and learn the difference.
* **Elyndar** — The people and the country. If the rolls say “Elyndar ruins” and “Glimmerhollow,” they are pointing at the same stones.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“You do not enter. You are suffered. If the path behind you is gone, that was a refusal, not a puzzle. Sit down and wait for dawn. Do not cut a tree to make a new path. The tree is someone’s cousin, and in that country the joke is not a joke.”*

---

### 🏞️ **Minor Settlements & Ruins**

1. **Fallowridge** — A farming village near Emberwood; often raided by goblins but known for its stubborn survivors. **Where:** eight leagues **due south** of Brackenford, one day on foot, on the ridge of the southeastern hem. The Ashfall Flats, and the wastes proper, begin **two leagues south** of the square. **What the year 1350 did:** destroyed it. **What 1384 did:** the survivors rebuilt a smaller village on the same cellars. The place in this list is the rebuilt one, alive in 1477. Alderman **Hob** runs the mill and the argument. **Nim** farms the ash-edge and has not lost a season to anything that was not on fire. Goblin raids are Firefang coming south and, some years, Bone-Spear coming north. Both like grain.
2. **Stonevein Outpost** — Already placed under the mountains. Dwarven mining station, partially abandoned after a dragon skirmish. Southwestern flank of Stonehollow, sixteen leagues south of Stonehold, eight days’ foothill-walk east of the Cavern, eight days from my door if you go northeast across the plains. Living, barely, and offended to be called minor.
3. **Glimmerhollow** — Already placed under the Glades. Elven ruins, two days inside the Veil. Not a living city. A living watchfulness.
4. **Blackspire Keep** — A ruined human fortress; said to have been built over a dragon’s ashfall. The ashfall was Myravelle’s. **Where:** six leagues **east** of Fallowridge, above the Mere of Cinders, on the basalt spike. From Brackenford, **ten leagues southeast**, a day and a morning on foot. It was raised in the early Third Age by a Vaelorin captain who thought a view of the wastes was a defense. The wastes agreed, and took the garrison. Ruin now. Bandit kings use it, the Cursed Knight has been seen on the causeway, and crypt-wraiths use the lower store. It stands between the plains and the ash, which is why both coats claim it and neither sweeps the floor.
5. **Moonshadow Camp** — Nomadic Free Tribes settlement, hidden from wandering armies. **Where:** on the south side of the border between the Veiled Glades and the Ashen Wastes, in a willow hollow. **Two days southeast** of the Glade-veil’s eastern edge. **Three days northwest** of the Obsidian Monoliths — fifteen leagues by the broken road — and those same fifteen leagues, three days, **southwest** of Fallowridge. The monoliths themselves sit twenty leagues, four days, almost due south of that village, so the hollow lies west and a little north of the ring. Do not draw this as a straight square; the ash does not do squares. **What 1350 did:** scoured the permanent camp. **What the tribes did:** lived, and by 1362 were raising the camp again each autumn, never the same tents twice. The place in this list is that seasonal raising. Mother-of-Ashes **Ruka** decides if it stands. In spring you will find scorched rings and no one to argue with.

### 🧭 *A Day’s Walk, Written So You Can Argue With Me*

If you trust nothing else, trust this chain. I have sent people along it and gotten some of them back.

* Brackenford south gate to Hearthmile Stone: four leagues south, half a day.
* Hearthmile to Ruinwatch: sixteen leagues west-northwest, two days afoot, one mounted.
* Ruinwatch to the Cavern mouth: eight leagues north, one day.
* Cavern mouth to Sky Pyre ridge: two leagues north, half a day scrambling.
* Brackenford to Fallowridge: eight leagues south, one day.
* Fallowridge to the ash lip: two leagues south.
* Fallowridge to Blackspire: six leagues east.
* Brackenford to the Ley-Line Pools: ten leagues southwest through trees, a day and a half.
* Brackenford to Firefang Shrine: twenty-two leagues west through timber, four days.
* Brackenford to Ashford: sixteen leagues east, one day mounted.
* Brackenford to Cindermark: forty-eight leagues east, three days mounted, six afoot.
* Brackenford to Stonevein: fifty leagues northeast, five days mounted, eight afoot.
* Cavern mouth to Stonevein along the foothills: eight days afoot, near fifty leagues.
* Saltmercy to the Isles: twelve days’ sail west.
* Brackenford to the Glade-veil: four days southwest along the southern eaves, twenty-four leagues.

A hundred leagues is farther than any single one of these. It is the width of the scream, not the width of a morning’s errand. Anyone who tells you the Cavern is “a hundred leagues off” has never paid a farrier.

---

### 🧩 *Tavernkeeper’s Reflection*

Ah, the lands of Hollowvale… they speak, if you know how to listen. Mountains hum with old fire, forests whisper with goblin plots, caverns breathe the memory of dragons, and the isles… well, they dream.

Mark these places, friend, for when you journey, every step could uncover history, magic, or death. And remember: the world remembers those who walk it — and it does not forget easily.

I have given you days and leagues because graves were filling up with people who had only poetry. Poetry is for after you arrive. If I am wrong by a league, the road will correct me, and you may correct me on your return, provided you return with the mug you borrowed.

Ah, very well, traveler. Now we delve into the stories that twist through time, whispered in the shadows, carried on the winds, and sung by drunken bards in taverns. These are the myths, legends, and prophecies that shape Hollowvale’s fate — and perhaps yours.

# 📘 *The Grand Chronicle of Hollowvale*

## **Part VIII: Myths, Legends, and Prophecies**

*As Told by the Keeper of the Embered Flagon*

---

### 🍻 *The Tavernkeeper Speaks*

Sit closer, friend. Lean in. Some of these tales are old enough to make the stones weep. They are not always true… but in Hollowvale, truth and fire are often the same thing. And remember, every prophecy has a way of finding the fool bold enough to believe it.

---

### 🐉 **Dragon Myths**

* **The First Flames**: Long ago, Aeltharion, Serenya, and Veyrath shaped the world. The dragons born from Veyrath’s sparks were more than beasts — they were living embodiments of divine power. Legends say that even when they sleep, their dreams ripple through ley-lines, influencing events unseen. The three makers are not the seven thrones. The thrones are children. The makers are the reason there is anything to burn.
* **The First Flame and the Last**: Veyrath was called the First Flame while he ruled the Sky Pyre. He is called, in prophecy and in cult-scripture, the Last Flame now that only the heart’s dream remains. If a text uses one title for the living king and the other for the apocalypse, it is being careful. If a bard uses them as rhymes, he is being paid by the line.
* **The Embered Flight**: Myth tells of a dragon named **Myravelle**, whose wings could stir winds that carried the scent of fire to every corner of Hollowvale. Sailors swear storms once howled in her laughter. Her ghost is the one reported over Emberwood. Her ashfall is the Mere under Blackspire.
* **The Gilded Maw**: Zhyranda, dragon of gold, is said to have breathed wealth and ruin alike, teaching mortals that greed and glory are often intertwined. Her essence is rumored to linger in buried treasure vaults, cursing those who seek it. The Cursed Crown is that rumor with a size.
* **Korvhal’s Towers**: The storm-dragon’s fortress fell inward, not out of the sky. That inward fall is the Ashen Ruins. Lightning still finds the tallest broken tooth of them in a clear summer, which the Firefang read as a letter.
* **Dragon Ghosts**: Travelers claim to see spectral forms of dragons in ash storms, glowing faintly and moving as though the world remembers them. In the wastes those are often Ashen Revenants wearing a larger shape. Sometimes they are not.
* **The Sleepers under the Isles**: The myth says dragons older than the Thrones sleep under Starfall and behind the Gates. Pell says the Peak was a watching-post. Both can be true if the watchers lay down at their work and have not heard the bell.

---

### 📜 **Legendary Deeds**

* **Lira of the Emberwood**: Archer who, with her dying shot, opened Veyrath’s heart-seam during the First Age, so that Seran’s sword could enter. Sacrificed her life to protect mortals. Her bow, *Ashstring*, vanished with her body. She is the first wraith, the shade in the eaves, not the armored knight who hunts sword-bearers.
* **Seran Blackforge**: Struck the killing blow along the arrow’s path. Vanished with the sword in the collapse of the year 500. The dwarves keep his hall-name in the Chainforge. They do not keep his bones.
* **Kaelen the Ash-Walker**: Knight cursed to wander ruins, forever chasing the Sword of Aeltharion. It is said that where his shadow falls, forgotten relics stir. Third Age. Still at it.
* **Durvak Stonevein**: Leader of the Ironborn dwarves; his hammer strikes are rumored to carry both metal and memory, as if each blow recalls the chains that once bound dragons. Called Durnak Ironborn by human scribes. The revolt was 1044. The curse is metal in the veins. One dwarf, two names, and a clan that did not end with him.
* **Serra of the Embered Veil**: Sorceress who claimed communion with the dragon’s heart. Her prophecies shaped the rise and fall of kingdoms in the Third Age. She spoke to the Last Flame and the listeners supplied the ambition.

---

### 🔮 **Prophecies of the Last Flame**

* *“When the Last Flame rises, the Sword shall awaken, and ash will speak the truth of the world.”* This is the line that spread again in the year **1378**, the Prophetic Resurgence, from cult-cells into princely courts. It had been whispered before. 1378 is when it became fashionable, which is more dangerous.
* *“The kingdoms will crumble, the chains will shatter, and goblin and man alike will kneel before fire reborn.”* Vaelorin has already done the crumbling on its own schedule, in 1423, and the princes were not kneeling. Prophecy loves to take credit for politics.
* *“Beware the hand that lifts the crown of Vaelorin; the wearer’s soul shall melt as iron in flame.”*
* *“In the forgotten isles, beneath starfall, the hearts of dragons wait, dreaming of reckoning.”*

Some scholars claim the prophecies are fragments of lost elven divinations, others whisper that they are remnants of dragon memory encoded in dreams. Regardless, every age finds one fool or hero who interprets them — and changes the world in the process.

A practical reading, which I offer because practical readings save stables: the Last Flame rising means the heart under the Cavern breaking the sealing-chains of 842. The sword awakening means Elarthyn, not the splinter. The Isles’ line means the sleepers, and it may mean the same fire seen from the west. None of these things have happened as of 1477. The hum you may hear in the Halls is the prelude bards keep mistaking for the downbeat.

---

### 🌌 **Omens and Signs**

* **Red Ash Storms**: When ash falls from the sky without fire, some say it is the warning of the Last Flame stirring. The 1350 cataclysm was the great instance. Lesser red snows have fallen since, mostly on the southern hem. A red morning over Fallowridge is a reason to shut the mill, not a reason to preach.
* **Dragon Dreams**: Children born under eclipses sometimes speak in tongues, describing landscapes and flames unknown to mortals. The cult collects them. Sella hides them when she can. If a child in Brackenford starts naming halls they have not walked, bring them to the shrine before you bring them to a stranger with clean hands.
* **The Singing Stones**: Rare minerals hum with faint voices; interpreted as messages from the First Flames or warnings of future disaster. Stonehold Deep is full of the dishonest kind, which hums because the ley is angry. The honest kind is rarer and ruder.
* **Molten Rivers**: Hidden veins of molten rock, appearing in unusual places, said to be the heartbeat of dragons beneath the earth. The Pyre Run and the rivers inside the Cavern are the heartbeat you can actually map. A new one opening in a field is an omen. A new one opening in the street is an evacuation.

---

### 🧩 *Tavernkeeper’s Reflection*

Ah, friend, these myths… they are more than stories. They are warnings, riddles, and memories all rolled into one. To ignore them is folly; to believe them without caution is madness. But those who study them, who listen to the whispers of ash, shadow, and stone… they see the threads connecting every hero, every artifact, and every faction.

When you walk Hollowvale, remember: the legends are not idle tales. They are alive. And the Last Flame? One day it will stir again. Perhaps you will witness it… or perhaps it will witness you.

# 📘 *The Grand Chronicle of Hollowvale*

## **Part IX: The Main Road, and the Side Quests**

*As Told by the Keeper of the Embered Flagon*

---

### 🍻 *The Tavernkeeper Speaks*

Ah, yes… side quests. The bread and butter of a life spent wandering, and the cause of many a broken mug here at the Flagon. These are not the sweeping deeds of kings and dragons, but the smaller, stranger, sometimes bloodier tales that color the world. The main road is sweeping enough. I put it here because this is the chapter where deeds are still ahead of you, not behind.

Every adventurer knows them, every bard sings a few, and every innkeeper swears some are true. Listen closely, friend — one day, one of these might find you.

The four callings that take this road — knight, mage, thief, and dwarf — are not a prophecy. They are what survives the first tavern bill. Go as you are. The road is not particular, and the goblins are less particular than the road.

---

### 🍺 **The Main Questline — The Road the Flagon Keeps**

This is the living road in the year 1477. It is not a history of the year 500. The god is already dead. You are not being sent to kill him again. You are being sent to walk his corpse, survive his heirs, and come back with the truth of how close the heart is to waking.

1. **The tavern gathering.** It starts in the Embered Flagon, at my bar, with the village around us: Hest’s market to the west of the square, Bramble’s forge, Sella’s shrine, Yselle’s militia pretending not to listen. You take the rumor — goblins on the Ember Road, a drake in the Halls, a hum in the stone that might be the sword. You take a healing potion or you regret it. The cellar is not this step. The cellar is for people who ask the right question later.
2. **The Road to the Cavern, and the ambushes.** Out the south gate. Four leagues to Hearthmile Stone, which Yselle still calls safe. Then the bend, west-northwest, sixteen leagues to Ruinwatch, and the wood closes. Firefang archers in the trees. Brutes where the track pinches, especially where the road comes abreast of the Ashen Ruins, half a day south of Ruinwatch in the trees. You can parley if Vesska’s mark is on you. You probably do not have Vesska’s mark yet. Three and a half days on foot to the mouth, two if you are mounted and the horse agrees to the last climb.
3. **The Cavern of Embers.** The mouth first. The secret in the upper face of the entrance is the Cinder Vault, if you are the sort who looks sideways: chests, two of them locked hard enough to want a thief’s picks, and the chance of the Star-Shard Splinter. The descent inward is the Molten Halls. Skrat’s fortifications. Goblins, brutes, archers. Molten river. Do not sprint.
4. **The dragon encounter.** At the bottom of the Halls an Ember Drake nests against the sealed stair. It is not Veyrath. It is a child of the heart’s heat, rare, fed by the Firefang as worship. Defeating it is the victory this road contains. The Heart Chamber stays sealed under the chains of 842. If you break that seal on your first visit you will not need a return scene, because there will not be a you to return.
5. **What you recover.** Not Elarthyn. A splinter that hums, if the Vault or the drake’s nest yields it, and a whisper that proves the true blade still drinks somewhere beneath. The sword’s whereabouts remain a mystery on purpose. Come back with the splinter and the news, not with a god’s rib in a sack.
6. **The return, and the longer shadow.** Back up the Ember Road to Brackenford. The Flagon will be standing unless it isn’t. What follows you home is not the drake. It is the cult’s interest, the dwarves’ question about any link you pocketed, and the Isles stirring in other people’s mouths. The greater threats were always behind the drake, not in it. You have seen the door. You have not opened the age.

The Three-Country Trail, off the mouth’s southeastern slope, is not the main road. It is where you go when you are not ready to go home and not ready to go deeper. Plains, emberwood, ash. Wilderness. It has eaten better plans than yours.

---

### 🐲 **The Goblin Warlord**

* **Hook**: Surviving goblins rally under a cunning leader named *Grishnak Flameeye*. Reports of raids and stolen relics rise daily. In 1477 this is the present tense.
* **Where**: His reach is the northern eaves, Fallowridge’s fields, and the Halls. He himself moves. Skrat is the one who stays.
* **Possible Encounters**:

  * Ambush on merchant caravans, especially the Dike between Brackenford and Lowford, and the grain-carts south to Fallowridge.
  * Diplomatic parley with goblin shamans. Vesska at Firefang Shrine, four days west through the timber.
  * Discovery of a hidden goblin shrine to a long-dead dragon. The public one is Firefang. The hidden one is a smoke-room under the Ashen Ruins, half a day south of Ruinwatch.
* **Rewards/Consequences**: Treasure, reputation, or enraged goblins seeking vengeance. A smoke-oath from Vesska will get you through one stretch of the Ember Road and will expire when that fire dies.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“Grishnak’s clever, I’ll give him that. But clever goblins are still goblins — and blood tastes the same to them, no matter who spills it.”*

---

### ⛓️ **The Broken Chains**

* **Hook**: Pieces of the Chains of the Shattered Flame resurface in distant ruins. Whoever collects them could wield them—or be crushed by their power.
* **Where the old oath scattered them**: under Stonehold, under Emberwood, and in the Cavern. One link is sworn, by a dwarf I believe, to lie in the collapsed deep of the 1289 battle. One is in the Cinder Vault’s locked chest more often than chance allows. One is somewhere under the northeastern eaves, and the Firefang have been using it as a holy irritant.
* **Challenges**: Traps, cursed guardians, moral dilemmas (do you reforge chains or destroy them?). Thane Molle wants them returned. The Order wants them unmade or re-hidden. Grishnak wants them because his shamans say the fire likes the smell.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“Chains aren’t just metal, friend… they’re memory, anger, and history all bound tight. Mishandle them, and they’ll bind *you*.”*

---

### 🧟 **The Cursed Knight**

* **Hook**: An undead wraith hunts those who bear the Sword of Aeltharion — and, in practice, those who bear the splinter, the crown, or any link that still remembers a dragon.
* **Who he is not**: He is not Lira. She is the bow-shade in the eaves. He is the black-armored oath of the second Order, born of the sword-curse that began in the First Age.
* **Mysteries**: Is he friend or foe? Can he be redeemed? He has warned companies off the crown and then killed the ones who took the warning as permission to take the sword instead. I call that a foe with standards.
* **Encounters**: Haunted ruins, spectral duels, cryptic prophecies. Blackspire’s causeway is the sighting I trust. The Ash Road is the other.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“I’ve seen him ride through snowstorms with armor black as midnight. A man once, now a warning. If he looks at your pack, drop the relic before you drop a speech.”*

---

### 🏝️ **The Forgotten Isles Expedition**

* **Hook**: Sailors whisper of sleeping dragons and sealed gates; explorers are sought to uncover secrets. The year 1402 already tried. Pell Seaburn, at Saltmercy, is the telling and the warning.
* **The road there**: West through Emberwood, six days, or the long way around; four days by the Coast Track to Saltmercy; twelve days’ sail west. Starfall’s eastern cove, then the Peak. Gate Isle a day south. Catacomb Isle a day south of that.
* **Hazards**: Storms, magical anomalies, rival treasure hunters, traps set by old dragon civilizations, and water that boils the pitch out of the hull.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“Some say the Isles are alive. If you see fire in the mist, don’t assume it’s a lighthouse.”*

---

### 🔮 **The Tavern’s Secret Cellar**

* **Hook**: Hidden chamber beneath The Embered Flagon contains relics and forgotten histories of dragon-slayers.
* **What I will admit is down there**: A fragment of Serenya’s tear, under the market-name Seranya if you are reading a label I did not write. Journals of the second Order, at least one of which mentions Warden Calveth’s predecessors. A false wall that has, twice, had ash on the wrong side. The Chalice is not supposed to be there. If you find it, you have found my mistake, and you will put it back.
* **Encounters**: Traps, old journals with forbidden knowledge, hidden cult remnants. The cult would love a cellar under the realm’s gossip-heart. I have swept them out once. Sweeping is not a permanent art.
* **Rewards**: Unique artifacts, lore, or clues to the Sword of Aeltharion. The clue is a direction, not a map with a cross. Anyone who paints a cross is selling the splinter twice.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“I only show it to those who ask the right question… and prove their heart’s steady. The question is not ‘where is the sword.’ I hear that one before the mug is full.”*

---

### ⚒️ **The Dwarven Debt**

* **Hook**: Stonehollow dwarves demand the return of the Sword or the Chains, claiming they were forged with their blood. The sword-claim is pride. The chain-claim is fair. Seran was theirs. The Chains were Thurnok’s extracted teaching and their hammer-work.
* **Where**: Stonevein if you are lucky, Stonehold if the thane has already heard your name, the Deep if you brought a link and a lie.
* **Challenges**: Diplomacy, negotiation, or siege. You will not win the siege. Molle has the mountain, and the mountain is in a bad mood until the end of time.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“Dwarves remember every insult, every slight… and they never forget a debt. Pay in links, in work, or in an honest ‘I do not have it.’ Never in a forgery. They can hear the forge that didn’t make it.”*

---

### 🔥 **The Ashen Prophecy**

* **Hook**: Cultists attempt to use the Sword to awaken the Last Flame. They do not have the sword. They have the invocation, Ilthara, and a talent for getting pieces of people who have stood near the splinter.
* **Where**: The temples two days east of the Monoliths. Cells closer than that, including, some seasons, a face in my taproom.
* **Encounters**: Rituals, traps, moral choices (stop them or manipulate the prophecy?). Manipulation is how Serra’s wars started. I recommend stopping, and then leaving, and then not writing down the chant you overheard.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“The cultists speak of rebirth, but fire reborn can consume everything… including you.”*

---

### 🌌 **Other Minor Tales & Quests**

1. **The Whispering Stones** — Listen to stones that hum messages from dragons or the First Flames. The honest stones show up in the Deep and, rarely, in the bed of the Pyre Run. The quest is to carry one to Sella or to Molle without deciding you are a prophet on the way.
2. **The Phantom Caravan** — A spectral trade route that appears once every hundred years, on the broken Ash Road. Not a plan. A calamity with inventory. If it appears off its century, the Last Flame is closer than I like.
3. **The Moonlit Grove** — Elven ruins that grant visions or curses under the full moon. One day east of Glimmerhollow. Go at the full moon or do not go. Ithrael will know either way.
4. **The Ironborn Relic Hunt** — Recover lost dwarven artifacts to gain their favor or wrath. Durvak’s personal hammer is the prize everyone names and nobody should touch. A Chainforge punch, a collar-key, a Stonevein seal — smaller things, real debts.
5. **The Emberwood Treasure Hunt** — Solve puzzles left by goblin shamans to locate hidden treasure. The puzzles start at Firefang Shrine and end, usually, in a root-cellar full of stolen Dike silver and one item that should not have been stealable. Vesska finds it funny. You might.

---

### 📍 **Hooks by Country**

So a map has teeth. Each of these is a point of interest, an encounter, and a thing worth pocketing. None of them is the sword.

* **Brackenford.** *The cellar stair, the forge-whisper, the south-gate contract.* Encounter: a cult cell in the taproom, or a militia argument that becomes a brawl if you take Grishnak’s offer in public. Loot: healing potions from Sella, an iron sword Bramble will not explain, Hest’s lockpicks if she likes you, the tear-fragment if you ask rightly. The market and the inn are the points on the village itself.
* **The Ember Road.** *Hearthmile Stone, the pinch by the Ruins, Ruinwatch.* Encounter: Firefang archers, then brutes. Loot: goblin smoke-charms, a caravan strongbox, occasionally a single chain-link the ambushers do not understand. The road itself is the point of interest. Walking it is the encounter.
* **Emberwood deep.** *Firefang Shrine, Ley-Line Pools, the smoke-room under the Ashen Ruins.* Encounter: Vesska’s parley or her pets; a ley-serpent at the pools; Korvhal’s lightning on the tallest tooth. Loot: a shaman puzzle-cache, a mana-drop from the pools that Sella should bottle and not you, drake scales in the west-deep.
* **The Cavern.** *Mouth, Cinder Vault, Molten Halls, the drake at the sealed stair.* Encounter: Skrat’s garrison, the Ember Drake. Loot: the Star-Shard Splinter, locked-chest gold, slag-glass that mages pay for, and nothing from the Heart Chamber because you are not opening it.
* **Sky Pyre ridge.** *The hanging stones, Vareth’s last walk, the Order’s door you will not find.* Encounter: a warden’s test, or nothing, which is worse. Loot: cloud-stone that floats a finger off the palm. Do not build with it indoors.
* **Fallowridge.** *The Ash-Year cellars, Nim’s ash-edge field, Hob’s mill.* Encounter: a raid from the south or from the wood. Loot: grain, a cellar-cache missed in 1350, and the gratitude of people who rebuild, which spends better than it sounds.
* **Blackspire and the Mere.** *The causeway, the lower crypt, the steaming water.* Encounter: a bandit king, crypt-wraiths, possibly the Cursed Knight. Loot: a garrison payroll that predates the ruin, a cloak-clasp of Vaelorin, and a lungful of ash if you swim the Mere.
* **The Ashen Wastes.** *Monolith ring, temple shelf, a broken Ash Road post, the Salt-Ash Sink.* Encounter: Bone-Spear, an ash storm with revenants in it, an ash-lurker under the crust, Ilthara if you were invited. Loot: obsidian ley-anchors small enough to carry and unwise enough to sell, cult foci you should break, Sink-salt that preserves meat and curses the stew.
* **Moonshadow.** *The autumn rings, Ruka’s fire.* Encounter: a test of whether you are an army. You are not, but arrive like one and the hollow is empty. Loot: a fire-oath, a pony if you paid, news of the Phantom Caravan. There is no chest. They move.
* **The Veiled Glades.** *The veil-edge, Glimmerhollow’s scriptorium, the Moonlit Grove.* Encounter: being suffered, or being turned around. Loot: a Grimoire fragment only if Ithrael means it; a vision, which you cannot put in a pack; moonstag antler, only if the stag is already dead of something that was not you.
* **Vaelorin.** *Cindermark’s bowl, Ashford’s toll, Giltmere’s vaults, Mourngate’s gate.* Encounter: princely “escorts,” a crown-hunter, a riot if two banners meet. Loot: seals, a false crown, and once a generation the true one, which you will refuse if you listened in Part V.
* **Stonehollow.** *Stonevein winch, Stonehold gate, Chaincollar, Thurnok’s Prison door.* Encounter: a debt-court, a hot collapse, a collar-warden. Loot: a returned link’s reward, deep-crystal that hums dishonestly, ale that counts as a ration. Durvak’s hammer is not the loot. It is the mistake.
* **The Ice-Spine.** *Wyrmgap’s west wall, Lake Merrow.* Encounter: Ice-Spine Wyverns, in pairs, which is the important half of the sentence. Loot: a rime-spine, worth a prince’s ransom and a porter’s strike. Take one spine, not the pair’s worth.
* **The Isles.** *Starfall cove and Peak, the Gates, the Sunken Catacombs.* Encounter: the crossing itself, then a sleeper’s dream, a sealed door, a drowned trap. Loot: star-metal splinters that are not *the* splinter, catacomb reliquaries, and a changed way of sleeping. Pell will take you. He will not fetch you.
* **The Three-Country Trail.** *The dirt at the mouth’s lower east.* Encounter: whichever coat you walk into — plains bandits, wood goblins, ash wolves — on the same afternoon if you are greedy with the compass. Loot: whatever that coat drops. The trail is how the wider country opens without a second map. Use it, and remember which way the mouth was.

---

### 🧩 *Tavernkeeper’s Reflection*

Ah, yes… these are the adventures that make Hollowvale… Hollowvale. Small in scale, but rich in danger, mystery, and the faint smell of dragon-ash. Some will bring gold, some glory, some your doom. But all leave stories — stories that end up here, in this tavern, spilled into mugs, etched in memory, and whispered to those who will listen.

The main road ends at my door if you do it right. I will know if you opened the stair, because the ash will arrive before you do.

# 📘 *The Grand Chronicle of Hollowvale*

## **Part X: Beasts, Monsters, and Magical Creatures**

*As Told by the Keeper of the Embered Flagon*

---

### 🍻 *The Tavernkeeper Speaks*

Ah, yes… creatures. Some are noble, some are vile, and some are cleverer than kings. You’ll meet them in forests, ruins, caverns, and even in dreams. I’ve seen adventurers bested by creatures smaller than a goblet… and I’ve seen dragons felled by clever hands. Listen, friend — know your enemy, or the enemy will know you.

I will give you the rolls as the rolls have always been given, and then I will tell you which ground each of them actually stands on. A beast without a country is a decoration. Decorations do not bite. These do.

---

### 🐉 **Dragons**

* **Veyrath, the First Flame, called in his after-death the Last Flame**: The great red dragon. Dead in the year 500. His body is the Cavern of Embers. Rumored to survive there, and the rumor means the heart’s dream under the sealed stair, not a king who will sit up and converse. Embodiment of untamed fire and ancient wrath. If your bestiary says “the Last Flame” and mine says “the First,” we are pointing at the same corpse at two hours of its story.
* **Myravelle, the Sky-Wind**: Blue dragon whose wings could stir storms. Ghostly sightings reported over Emberwood, above the canopy, more often over the eastern and northern eaves than the deep. Her ash is at Blackspire, but her ghost prefers the trees. She does not land. If she lands, leave.
* **Zhyranda, the Gilded Maw**: Golden dragon; wealth, greed, and ruin follow her memory. No body on the map. A presence in Giltmere’s vaults, in Cindermark’s rubble, and in any hoard that kills its finder with wanting.
* **The sleepers**: Not named among the Seven. Under the Isles, if the myth and Pell agree. Do not add them to a hunting list.
* **Behavior & Notes**: Dragons are intelligent, cunning, and prideful. They remember mortals who wrong them and will test both courage and cleverness. The ones still able to do so are drakes, wyverns, ghosts, and a heart. The age of a dragon in the sky over Brackenford is over until the prophecy says it isn’t.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“I’ve smelled Veyrath’s smoke once… in my dreams. And I’ll tell you, it lingers for a lifetime. The Halls smell like that dream did. You will understand by the second bridge.”*

---

### 🐲 **Wyverns & Drakes**

* **Firefang Drakes**: Goblin-worshipped mini-dragons; fierce, territorial, and loyal to their clans. They lair in the western deep of Emberwood, around the approaches to the shrine, and the Firefang consider stealing an egg both a rite and a cause of war.
* **The Ember Drake**: Named again among the rare creatures, and it belongs here in the family. One nests in the Cavern, against the sealed stair. It is not a Firefang pet in the way a hound is a pet. It is a fire they feed.
* **Ice-Spine Wyverns**: Rare, frost-breathing drakes in the northern peaks. They hunt **in pairs**. Their ground is the Ice-Spine: the west walls of Wyrmgap, the high faces above it, and the air over Lake Merrow. They are not in Stonehollow’s forges and they are not in the wood. If you see one south of the foothills, it is lost or the world is.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“Don’t mistake size for danger. The smallest drake can take a man’s hand if he isn’t careful. A wyvern pair can take the rest of the party while you watch the first one.”*

---

### 🧟 **Undead & Cursed Beings**

* **Lira’s shade**: The first wraith. The eaves of Emberwood, especially where yew stands. Not a duel. A presence, and sometimes a warning shot that does not quite loose. Do not hunt her.
* **The Cursed Knight**: Wraith bound to the sword-curse of Elarthyn; hunts bearers of legendary weapons. Blackspire’s causeway, the Ash Road, and any camp foolish enough to lay the splinter on a table. Armor black. No bow.
* **Ashen Revenants**: Ghosts of those consumed by the Last Flame’s lesser angers, and by the year 1350 in particular. They linger near molten ruins and in ash storms. The Wastes in a storm are a city of them. The Mere of Cinders has a few who never finished drowning.
* **Crypt Wraiths**: Found in forgotten tombs; may curse intruders with visions of death or madness. Blackspire’s lower store. The Sunken Catacombs. Certain side-niches of the Cinder Vault if you open the wrong chest first. Cindermark’s undercrofts.
* **Kaelen the Ash-Walker**: Not a beast. I list him because he moves like one and the bestiary is where frightened people look. Ruins, the Ash Road, and my bad nights.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“Undead don’t fight fair… and some of them are cleverer than the living. The Knight is the clever one. The revenants are the weather. Dress for the weather. Negotiate with neither.”*

---

### 🐾 **Magical Beasts**

* **Shadowfang Wolves**: Hunters of the Ashen Wastes and of Emberwood; move like smoke, strike silently. In the wood they run the northern and southern hems. In the wastes they run the flats and the broken road. A pair has been seen on the Three-Country Trail where ash-fingers meet grass. They do not like fire. They like the moment after fire, when you think you are safe.
* **Moonlight Stags**: Rare, ethereal deer; their antlers carry faint magic that can amplify spells or prophecies. The Veiled Glades, and the Moonlit Grove above any other acre. Killing one to harvest an antler is how a mage becomes a story Ithrael tells as a warning. An antler from a stag dead of other causes is still a negotiation.
* **Ley-Line Serpents**: Snakes that dwell along magical leylines; their venom disrupts spells. The Pools. The monolith ring. The roots of Glimmerhollow. The hot cracks of the Ember Peaks. Their venom is why a mage goes quiet and then goes wrong. Do not put it in the thief’s vial and call it an upgrade.
* **Cinderboars**: Not noble enough for the old rolls, and common enough that the old rolls were proud. Tusks stained by mineral springs. The Bracken Plains, Fallowridge’s fields, the eastern eaves. They charge straight, which is a kindness, and they weigh as much as a debt. Hide and tusk are honest loot. Hest buys both and complains.
* **Ash-lurkers**: Things that lie under the waste-crust and rise when a storm thins the ground. The central wastes, especially a league out from the monoliths, where the ley gives them dreams. They are not wyrms. They are what the sand is doing when it has decided to have limbs.
* **Veilmoths**: Pale moths of the Glades, dust on the wing. The dust is a vision if you breathe it and a lost afternoon if you don’t. Ithrael’s people wear cloth over the mouth in the season. You should too.
* **Boilfin**: Fish of the Boiling Reach that swim the hot shallows off Gate Isle and Starfall’s lee. Sailors call a boilfin in the pot a blessing and a boilfin on the deck a warning that the water has come up to look. They are food until they are an omen. Pell will not eat them. That is enough for me.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“Magic makes beasts unpredictable. Respect them, or they’ll carve lessons into your flesh. The boar is the one that will kill you while you watch the sky for dragons. I have buried that joke with the man who learned it.”*

---

### 🐹 **Humanoid Threats**

* **Goblin Tribes**: Firefang in Emberwood and the Cavern; Bone-Spear in the Wastes; Night-Gleam in the ruin-margins and along the Veil. Cunning, organized, worshippers of fire or of dragon spirits. On the road and in the Halls you will meet them as rank-and-file, as **archers**, and as **brutes**. Those are war-roles, not three species. The brute is still a goblin. He is simply the goblin who ate better and stood in front.
* **Bandit Kings**: Human or halfling raiders in ruins or forests; often lead small armies of outlaws. Blackspire is the current throne of whichever one has not died this season. The eaves between Hearthmile and the deep wood grow a lesser crop of them. Dike-folk bandits prefer the road east of Lowford and will leave you your boots.
* **Cultists**: Ashen Cult operatives using minor magics, fire rituals, and illusions. The temples, the monolith approaches at night, and any town with a cellar. Brackenford included, over my broom.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“Clever creatures, humans and goblins alike. Sometimes their craftiness outshines brute strength. The brute, lowercase, is still stronger than you. Plan accordingly.”*

---

### 🐾 **Rare & Legendary Creatures**

* **The Ember Drake**: Born from molten rock and dragon flame; extremely rare. The one I will swear to lives in the Cavern of Embers, at the sealed stair. If there is a second, it is deeper than the stair, and it is not your business.
* **The Ash Wyrm**: Ancient serpentine beast; feeds on ley-line energy, leaving ruins in its wake. The deep wastes, and the line between the monoliths and the temples. It is not the lurker. The lurker is a local sin. The wyrm is a pilgrimage of appetite. When it has fed, the Ash Road loses another mile of posts.
* **The Flame-Horned Stag**: Herald of the Last Flame; sightings considered omens of great events. Not the moonlight stag. Larger, wronger, antlers like banked coals. Seen at the edge of Moonshadow in bad autumns, and once, in 1378, at the ash lip south of Fallowridge, which is how the resurgence got its second wind. If you see it, mark the date. Do not mark the stag. It has already marked you.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“These beasts don’t make themselves known lightly. If you see one, mark it — and pray it doesn’t mark you back.”*

---

### 🌍 **Where They Stand, for the Ranger Who Will Not Read Upward**

* **Brackenford and the plains**: cinderboars, ordinary wolves, Dike-road bandits, the rare cultist, goblin scouts at the eaves. No dragons. No wyverns. If someone sells you a wyvern-hunt on the Dike, he is selling the walk home.
* **Emberwood**: Firefang in all three roles, Firefang Drakes in the west, Shadowfang Wolves on the hems, ley-serpents at the Pools, Lira’s shade, Myravelle overhead, cinderboars at the eastern edge.
* **The Cavern and Sky Pyre**: Skrat’s goblins, the Ember Drake, molten things that are not quite beasts and still bite, crypt-wraiths in the Vault’s bad chests, revenants near any open vent. The heart is not a creature you can put on a table.
* **The Wastes, Blackspire, the Ash Road**: Bone-Spear, Shadowfang Wolves, ash-lurkers, Ashen Revenants, the Ash Wyrm, cultists, the Cursed Knight, crypt-wraiths in the keep.
* **Moonshadow and the southern hem**: Gorn, who are people, and the Flame-Horned Stag when the year is wrong. Wolves where the ash starts.
* **The Glades**: moonlight stags, veilmoths, ley-serpents at the roots, Night-Gleam only on the outside of the Veil. The Elyndar are not a bestiary entry. I am putting the sentence here so a ranger does not file them under quarry.
* **Stonehollow**: less beast than grievance. Ley-serpents in the hot cracks, deep-crystal that is not alive and behaves as if it is, the warmth in Thurnok’s Prison. A chained brood-remnant under the Ember Peaks, if the Binding slips. You do not hunt that. You run, and then you tell Molle, and then you keep running.
* **The Ice-Spine**: Ice-Spine Wyverns, in pairs. Little else that is not weather.
* **The Isles and the Reach**: boilfin, sleepers, whatever the Gates are keeping on which side. I will not invent a menagerie for a sea I have not sailed. Pell speaks of white shapes in the mist the size of hulls. I have written “sleepers” and I will stop.

---

### 🌌 *Tavernkeeper’s Reflection*

Ah, yes… these creatures, friend, are the world’s memory made flesh, fur, scale, or smoke. They remember dragons, heroes, and fools alike. Every encounter teaches something: courage, fear, cunning, or humility.

A wise adventurer treats beasts as both threat and teacher. And remember this — sometimes, the greatest danger isn’t the claws or fire… but the story you carry after you survive.

Ah, excellent, friend. Now we delve into the threads that twist reality itself — the rituals, spells, and magics that shape the very fabric of Hollowvale. Some are subtle, some are catastrophic, and some… well, they will follow you home whether you like it or not.

# 📘 *The Grand Chronicle of Hollowvale*

## **Part XI: Rituals, Spells, and Magical Practices**

*As Told by the Keeper of the Embered Flagon*

---

### 🍻 *The Tavernkeeper Speaks*

Ah, yes… magic. Some call it power, some call it heresy, and some… I call it trouble with a capital T. I’ve seen spells that sing like birds, curses that gnaw at bones, and rituals that make the stars themselves tremble. Listen well — knowledge of these things can save your life… or damn it.

---

### 🔥 **Dragon Magic**

* **Ley-Line Communion**: Ritual where mortals tap into a dragon’s lingering essence in ley-lines. Amplifies fire magic and elemental perception. The Pools, the monoliths, Glimmerhollow’s roots, and the air above the sealed stair are where the communion is loudest. The Ember Peaks are where it has been gagged.
* **Draconic Binding**: Ancient incantation to subdue or communicate with dragons; requires rare components and extreme focus. Ardent spoke the first of these words. The Chains are the words made iron. Doing it with a voice alone, in 1477, is how a mage becomes a brief torch.
* **Emberfire Glyphs**: Sigils left by dragons that can ignite spontaneously, burn curses, or protect treasures. Korvhal’s ruins are written in them. So is the door of the Heart Chamber, under the later chains. A glyph that protects treasure will not protect you from the treasure’s owners.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“Dragon magic doesn’t forgive mistakes. One slip, and your tongue might turn to ash before your body follows.”*

---

### 🕯️ **Ashen Cult Rites**

* **The Last Flame Invocation**: Attempt to awaken dormant dragons or catalyze apocalypse-level fire. Requires the Sword of Aeltharion and a rare celestial alignment. They have the alignment charts. They do not have the sword. The invocation without the sword is still enough to empty a village, as several ash-circles prove.
* **Ashen Bonding**: Sacrificing a creature or mortal to gain temporary control over fire or spirits. This is murder with a syllabus. Ilthara’s temples practice it. If you are the syllabus, the loot is not worth the reading.
* **Smoke Divination**: Interpreting ash clouds to predict political upheavals or adventurer movements. Vesska does a cousin of this for the Firefang, and the cult hates that a goblin does it better. Red ash without fire is the omen-form. Ordinary campfire smoke is how they find you anyway.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“I’ve smelled these rituals from leagues away. A fool thinks the smoke is harmless; it isn’t. Sulfur, old parchment, and a sweetness like burned honey. Leave the room.”*

---

### 🧝 **Elven and Ancient Magics**

* **Ley-Line Manipulation**: Drawing on ley-lines to enhance spells or create zones of power. Often dangerous if disrupted. The Elyndar do this the way you do breathing. A human doing it at the Pools is a guest who has started rearranging the walls.
* **Veil-Walking**: Briefly slipping into parallel dimensions or dreams to spy, escape, or commune with spirits. This is how the Glades can be four days from my door and still not where you left them. Ithrael’s people walk the Veil. You, if you try, will walk into a story and come out at the wrong ford.
* **Nature’s Embrace**: Healing, growth, and elemental control through communion with natural spirits. Sella’s shrine-work is the small, human, honest cousin of this. It makes healing potions and keeps the ford-shrine from cracking. It does not regrow a forest. The elves did that with the Glades, and it took them the whole Second Age and the Veil besides.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“The elves weave spells like a spider weaves webs — beautiful, intricate… and deadly if you stumble into them.”*

---

### ⚔️ **Mortal Spellcraft**

* **Runic Forging**: Imbuing weapons or armor with spells, often requiring rare metals, dragon essence, or moonlight. Bramble can do the small version, a rune that keeps an edge. The Chainforge does the version that keeps a mountain. Do not ask Bramble for the second. He will try, and the forge will tell on him.
* **Elemental Infusions**: Fire, ice, wind, or earth magically attached to weapons, sometimes enhancing, sometimes corrupting. A rime-spine from a wyvern takes ice the way a gutter takes rain. Dragon-slag takes fire and then takes the hand.
* **Ward Creation**: Protective glyphs for buildings, treasures, or towns. Yselle’s palisade has three, and one of them is real. The Cinder Vault’s locked chests have wards under the locks. Picks alone will not do for the worst of them; picks and a dispel-word will. The word is in the cellar journals, which is a reason to be polite to me.
* **The battle-charms a company drills**, because a grimoire is a poor thing to hold while a brute is walking over: a **firebolt** from the staff; a **frost nova** when they are too close; a thin **arcane shield**; and, for the mage who has been trusted with the spellbook’s later leaves, a **lightning bolt**, a **mana shield**, a **blink** that will put you in a wall if you panic, and **life leech**. Life leech drinks a wound to fill the caster. It is taught on the Dike in a whisper. It is one step from the forbidden, and the step is shorter than the page.
* **What is not magic, and still keeps you alive**: the knight’s shield-bash, the stance, the power strike; the thief’s backstab, the smoke, the picks; the dwarf’s cleave, the stone-skin breath, the roar that steadies a line. I list them so no one tells you the warrior is the one without a craft. Their craft is not getting enchanted to death before the mage finishes a sentence.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“Mortals’ magic is like their ale: sometimes strong, sometimes bitter, and sometimes it blows up in your face. I prefer the ale. The ale has never blinked me into the cellar.”*

---

### 📜 **Forbidden & Rare Practices**

* **Soul Binding**: Capturing a soul in objects — dangerous, illegal in every principality that still bothers to write laws, and often cursed. The cult’s bonding flirts with it. The Chains, at their worst, are a cousin: not a soul in a bottle, but a will in a link. Thurnok’s teaching crossed the line and the dwarves have been living on the far side of it, uncomfortably, ever since.
* **Chronomancy Fragments**: Minor time manipulation; can accelerate, slow, or echo moments, but risks unraveling sanity. Ardent’s notes, if the Grimoire holds them, are the source. Glimmerhollow is where those notes bite. I have seen a man live the same toast three times and weep on the fourth. He is why I do not keep that page upstairs.
* **Blood of the First Flames**: Using remnants of dragon or divine blood to supercharge spells. Highly unstable. The Pyre Run is blood, in the literal telling, and using it as a reagent is exactly as wise as that sounds. Serenya’s tear is the other blood, the wept kind, and it heals rather than arms. Do not mix them. I should not have to say that, and yet the cellar has a stain that says someone needed to hear it.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“I’ve seen men try to bind souls for coin. The last one is still screaming in the cellar — though perhaps that was the wine talking. It was not the wine. I am keeping the joke because the truth empties the room, and I have a business.”*

---

### 🌌 *Tavernkeeper’s Reflection*

Magic, friend, is a conversation with the world itself. It rewards precision, courage, and respect — but punishes hubris and greed. A spell can turn the tide of battle, unravel a kingdom, or burn your hair off while you read the incantation.

And remember this: in Hollowvale, magic remembers. The earth, the dragons, the ash… they all recall your touch. So study well, act cautiously, and never, ever trust a spell without understanding it.

# 📘 *The Grand Chronicle of Hollowvale*

## **Part XII: Chronicles of Notable Events and Historical Conflicts**

*As Told by the Keeper of the Embered Flagon*

---

### 🍻 *The Tavernkeeper Speaks*

Ah, history… a bitter draught, stronger than the finest ale. Remember this, traveler: the past is alive here. Kings rise, dragons fall, cults stir, and every stone you walk over remembers the deeds of those who came before. Heed the chronicles, for they whisper warnings — and sometimes, opportunities.

All years below are the **Embered Reckoning**, counted from the Gift of Steel. The **Crown Count** of Vaelorin sets its Year 1 at our year 500. When an old annal says the Dragon-King fell in the year 842, it has added Crown Year 342 to the epoch and called two different hours the same hour. I will write both hours. You will not have to choose which fire you believe.

---

### 🗡️ **The First Age — Dawn of Flame (0–500 years)**

* **Year 0 — The Gift of Steel**: Aeltharion’s secret, in a village the dwarves and the humans still argue over. The reckoning begins.
* **Dragon Dominance**: Dragons openly ruled the land, demanding tribute and shaping early civilization. The Seven Pyric Thrones divided the world by the reach of wings.
* **First Villages**: Mortals built under dragon protection; society structured around tribute, survival, and dragon worship.
* **Year 247 — The Forging of the Sword of Aeltharion**: Seran Blackforge, under a red eclipse. Star-shard from Starfall Isle, quenched in a dying dragon’s heart. Named Elarthyn by him and the Sword by everyone else.
* **Year 347 — The Ember Siege**: Villages burned by rogue dragons along the eastern eaves; the first coordinated mortal resistance. Fire teaches lessons in pain. Many never forgot.
* **Year 365 — The Order’s first founding**: Seran, Lira, and Ardent raise the banner of the Shattered Flame on the cinders of Veilroot. Creed, not college. *“Better to die by fire than live in its shadow.”*
* **The burning of Veilroot**: In the War of Tribute the southern wood became ash. That scar is why the Wastes border Emberwood, and why the Glades and the great wood are two survivors of one forest.
* **Year 500 — The Fall of Sky Pyre**: Ardent bends the ley-lines. Lira’s arrow opens the seam. Seran’s sword follows it into Veyrath’s heart. The scream carries a hundred leagues and stops at the sea. The body becomes the Cavern of Embers. The fortress falls onto the ridge two leagues north of the mouth. The sword vanishes. Seran vanishes. Ardent is ash. Lira becomes the first wraith. The first Order’s temples become tombs. The First Age ends in ruin, not triumph.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“Even back then, mortals were clever, but fire teaches lessons in pain. The Fall is the year 500. If a song gives it to 842, the song is wearing Vaelorin’s crown and hoping you cannot add.”*

---

### ⛓️ **The Second Age — Age of Chains (500–1200 years)**

* **512–540**: The surviving great dragons retreat, sleep, or die. Drakes and wyverns inherit the leftovers.
* **Year 547 — The Goblin Exodus**: Firefang into the regrowing wood, Bone-Spear into the ash of Veilroot, Night-Gleam into the ruins.
* **Year 560**: Human moot at the Bracken ford. A camp, not yet Brackenford.
* **Year 588**: King Maelor the Chain-Sworn proclaims the Kingdom of Vaelorin on Zhyranda’s old ground.
* **Year 640 — The Order born again**: Dame Ossethe and Bramm Blackforge refound the Order as a college of keepers. This is the birth the later faction-rolls mean. The heroes on the charter are the dead of the first founding, kept on the roll, not present in the room.
* **Year 688**: The Chains of the Shattered Flame are finished under Thurnok’s compelled guidance. Thurnok dies in the prison-hall. The links are scattered by oath under Stonehollow, Emberwood, and the Cavern.
* **710–800 — The Chaining Wars**: The profession of binding. Zhyranda’s last breath is caught in the gold that becomes the Cursed Crown. Cindermark rises.
* **Year 842 — The Sealing of the Heart**: Crown Year 342. The Order and Vaelorin’s knights enter the Cavern and chain the heart. They proclaim the Dragon-King defeated. He has been dead since 500. The fortress has already fallen. The sword is not found, because it was lost in 500. The damaged annal that writes “Dragon-King Veyrath Defeated (Year 842)” and then “fortress collapsed, Sword lost” has stacked the death onto the sealing. Read it as the political victory. The corpse is three hundred and forty-two years older than the boast.
* **Year 899 — The Binding of Ember Peaks**: Dwarves drive enchanted chains into the cores of the Ember Peaks, inside Stonehollow, by way of Chaincollar Pass. A different work from the scattered Chains.
* **Year 940**: The Elyndar drop the Veil and leave Cindermark’s court.
* **Year 1044 — The Ironborn Revolt**: Durvak Stonevein, called Durnak Ironborn on human writs, leads the deep clans against a Vaelorin seizure of the Chains. Metal enters his veins. “Last of the Ironborn” is an epithet, not a census. The clan continues.
* **Year 1104–1130**: Vareth the Pale preaches. In 1130 he enters the Sky Pyre ruins and does not leave.
* **Year 1170**: A peak-binding cracks. A chained brood-dragon burns a procession before the collars take it again.
* **Year 1200**: Vaelorin decrees dragons a myth. The decree is a lie and a birthday. The Third Age begins.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“Chains don’t last forever, friend. They hold today, break tomorrow, and the world remembers every fracture. The Sealing holds in 1477. I dislike how much of my business depends on that remaining true.”*

---

### 🕯️ **The Third Age — Age of Ash, called the Age of Shadows (1200 to the present; a tidy fool wrote 2100 as an imagined end)**

* **Dragon Myth to Legend**: Dragons fade into story. What remains hides as a heart in the Cavern, as sleepers in the Forgotten Isles, as ghosts over the wood, and as wyverns in the northern peaks. The decree says none of this. The decree is wrong.
* **Year 1213 — Brackenford Founded**: At the old ford, around the Embered Flagon. Hub of adventurers, merchants, and scholars who drink.
* **Years 1256–1263 — The Emberwood Skirmishes**: Below, in the conflicts.
* **Rise of Goblin Tribes**: Firefang, Bone-Spear, and Night-Gleam assert themselves in the forests, the wastes, and the ruins. By the late fourteen-hundreds, Grishnak Flameeye is the warlord the whispers mean.
* **Year 1289 — The Battle of Stonehollow**: Below.
* **Year 1325 — Ashen Cult Emergence**: The secretive sect forms in the Ashen Wastes, scripture of Vareth, seat that will be the temples east of the monoliths. They seek to awaken the Last Flame.
* **Year 1350 — The Ashfall Cataclysm**: Below. Fallowridge destroyed and later rebuilt. Moonshadow’s permanent camp destroyed; the seasonal camp returns.
* **Year 1378 — The Prophetic Resurgence**: *“When the Last Flame rises, the Sword shall awaken”* spreads across the kingdoms. A Flame-Horned Stag is seen at the ash lip south of Fallowridge.
* **Year 1402 — The Forgotten Isles Expedition**: Below.
* **Year 1423 — The fall of Cindermark**: The central citadel of Vaelorin falls. The Cursed Crown vanishes. Seven principalities remain, and no king.
* **Year 1477 — The present telling**: I am pouring. The stair under the drake is still sealed. The sword is still unfound. Ilthara still has eyes like coals. You are still sitting too close to the story.
* **Tavernkeeper Note**: *“Ash falls where fire once ruled. The past here is a shadow, and shadows stretch long. We are not in the year 2100. Anyone who dates a contract so is selling you a century he does not own.”*

---

### ⚔️ **Notable Conflicts & Cataclysms**

1. **The Emberwood Skirmishes (Year 1256–1263)**

   * Goblin tribes against Brackenford’s new militias. The forest becomes a battleground, mostly the eastern and northern eaves.
   * Remnants of dragon shrines burned or repurposed. Firefang keeps the shrine in the west-deep through the whole ugly decade.
   * Tavernkeeper Note: *“I’ve seen old scars in the woods; they still smell of smoke. Yselle’s grandmother fought in the last year of it and never again ate mushrooms.”*

2. **The Battle of Stonehollow (Year 1289)**

   * Dwarves repel rogue dragons — lesser brood, not the Seven — seeking the Chains of the Shattered Flame.
   * Massive underground collapse; some tunnels still unexplored. Stonevein Outpost is partially abandoned from this year on, and has never decided to finish the job of leaving.
   * Tavernkeeper Note: *“Molle still pays a watch to stand on a winch above a hole. That is either duty or grief. In dwarves the difference is a matter of bookkeeping.”*

3. **The Ashfall Cataclysm (Year 1350)**

   * Mysterious eruption of ley-line energy in the Ashen Wastes; the cults come out of it stronger.
   * The blast rolls north across the border the burned Veilroot left between the wastes and the wood.
   * Villages destroyed, including the minor settlements of Fallowridge and Moonshadow Camp.
   * Fallowridge is rebuilt on the same ridge by the year 1384, smaller, stubborn, still raided, alive in the region-roll because the region-roll describes the living village. The Ash-Year cellars are still under Hob’s mill.
   * Moonshadow’s people are not destroyed with the tents. By 1362 they raise the camp again each autumn, hidden, seasonal. The region-roll’s Moonshadow is that camp. Both sentences — destroyed, and standing — are true, and they are true of a fire and of an autumn, not of two different maps.
   * Tavernkeeper Note: *“I poured for the refugees and I pour for their grandchildren. If you call Fallowridge a ruin in front of Nim she will put a hoe through your theory.”*

4. **The Forgotten Isles Expedition (Year 1402)**

   * Attempted recovery of dragon relics; most adventurers vanished or returned changed.
   * Starfall Peak recorded as an ancient dragon observation post, which it also is: the strike-place of the sword’s star, and the ridge where the blade was glimpsed the night of the Fall.
   * Captain Pell Seaburn came back to Saltmercy. The Gates stayed shut. The catacombs stayed drowned. The sleepers, if they rolled over, did not introduce themselves.
   * Tavernkeeper Note: *“Twelve days west, and most of them did not get twelve days back. Curiosity is a fine thing in a taproom and a fatal thing in hot water.”*

5. **The Fall of Cindermark (Year 1423)**

   * Not a dragon. A crown, a citadel, and the greed the crown was forged to feed. The center of Vaelorin collapses in a night.
   * The Cursed Crown is last seen here and then nowhere I can put on a bill.
   * Seven princes. The Third Age continues, kingless in the east, still pouring in the west.
   * Tavernkeeper Note: *“They built a throne on a dragon’s last breath and acted surprised when it bit. I was not surprised. I raised the price of ale a penny and called it mourning.”*

---

### 🌌 *Tavernkeeper’s Reflection*

Ah, you see, traveler… history isn’t neat. It’s a trail of ash, blood, and fire. Heroes are remembered for deeds, fools for mistakes, and even dragons leave marks that centuries cannot erase.

If you walk Hollowvale, remember the past whispers. The ruins, forests, and mountains are alive with memory. And sometimes, if you listen closely in a tavern late at night… the world itself will tell you secrets that aren’t written anywhere else.

The secrets that *are* written here took me long enough. Do not let a shorter copy send you to the year 842 to watch a fortress fall. Go to the ridge north of the Cavern if you want the stones, and go to the sealed stair if you want the chains, and know which century your feet are in.

# 📘 *The Grand Chronicle of Hollowvale*

## **Conclusion — Reflections of the Tavernkeeper**

---

### 🍻 *The Tavernkeeper Speaks*

Ah… so you’ve made it through all of it. The tales, the battles, the magic, the creatures, the prophecies… and the tavern itself, where all of it begins and ends. Hollowvale is more than land and sky, more than kingdoms and ruins; it is memory, woven into stone, ash, and flame. Every hero, every villain, every goblin and dwarf leaves a mark. Every blade forged, every spell cast, every shadow and fire-scorched footprint carries a story.

To walk Hollowvale is to walk history itself. And remember this: in every shadow, every flicker of flame, every distant roar in the mountains… the world remembers you. It watches, tests, and sometimes rewards those clever, bold, or foolish enough to leave their own story.

You have the years in one line now. You have the roads in leagues. You have the two births of the Order, the two names of the dwarf, the two titles of the dragon, and the two truths of Fallowridge — burned, and rebuilt — and of Moonshadow — scoured, and raised again each autumn. You have the south gate, and the knowledge that south is only the first hour of a road that finds the Cavern in the north. If a shorter chronicle argues with this one, the shorter chronicle is where the pages fell out. I put the Second Age back where the telling always said it stood. I did not move the First, and I did not flatter the Third.

---

### 🌌 **The Essence of Hollowvale**

* **Time is Layered**: Ages overlap. The First Flames still breathe through ley-lines. The chains of the past echo in every cavern. Legends of dragons, heroes, and cults are never truly gone. The year 500 is still happening in the shape of the ground. The year 842 is still happening in the iron around the heart. The year 1350 is still happening in Hob’s cellar and in Ruka’s decision to raise the tents.
* **Every Choice Matters**: From a goblin skirmish to wielding the Sword of Aeltharion, actions ripple through history. Prophecies may warn, but the brave or reckless shape their own fates. The main road offers you a drake and a splinter. The stair offers you the age. Choose with your eyes open, which is all I can pour you besides the ale.
* **Adventure Awaits**: Even in small villages, dark forests, or forgotten ruins, the world holds tales yet untold. There is always a quest, a secret, or a creature waiting to test those daring enough to seek it. The Isles are twelve days west of Saltmercy and a lifetime west of ready. The Unmarched East can wait. So can the ice beyond Merrow. I have not mapped what I have not, in some fashion, been brought news of. Let that honesty be the edge of the map.

---

### 🧩 *Tavernkeeper’s Final Reflection*

Remember, friend… Hollowvale is alive. Not just the lands or the dragons, but the stories themselves. They breathe in the wind, whisper in the fires, and hum beneath your feet. Respect them, learn from them, and perhaps… leave a tale worth telling in your own right.

So raise your mug one last time. To the dragons, the heroes, the ashes, and the flames. And to the world that never forgets those who dare to step into it.

The south gate is down the square. Hearthmile Stone is four leagues on. I will keep the hearth. Bring the mug back. If you cannot, send word by someone who can, and I will add you to the ledger in ink instead of in ash.

*May your paths be perilous, your choices bold, and your stories worthy of the Embered Flagon.*
